## Answered

| # | Question | Answer | Answered |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do the BC Laws (King's Printer) terms let us store and show verbatim consolidated statute text inside a commercial product? What attribution and disclaimer wording is required? | Yes. The King's Printer Licence – British Columbia v1.1 allows commercial use, copying, publishing and inclusion in our own product, on condition that we keep the text accurate and current, keep notices intact, do not suggest official status, and prominently display the attribution statement recorded in `licensing-register.md`. Placement of that statement is open question 9. | 2026-09-30 |
| 2 | Do CRT decision reuse terms permit storing full text, or only summaries plus a link? Are there restrictions on showing party names? | No reuse licence was found, so treat CRT decisions as all rights reserved: citation, link and our own reviewed summary only, and no party names, until the CRT answers (item 19). The CRT names parties by default and can anonymize or remove information later. Ownership is open question 10. | 2026-09-30 |
| 3 | Same question for BC Supreme Court and Court of Appeal judgments, including how to detect and respect publication bans. | The courts require written permission for use in a user-pay legal research tool. Until granted: citation, link and our own reviewed summary only, no text stored (item 18). Publication bans: exclude banned material at curation time; how to detect bans in bulk is part of the permission application. | 2026-09-30 |
