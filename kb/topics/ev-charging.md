# EV charging in a strata building

## Summary

An owner has a statutory route to install charging at their stall that the bylaws cannot close: a
request to the strata corporation to approve the alterations to common property needed for EV
charging infrastructure [bc.spa.s90.1]. The corporation **must not unreasonably refuse** it, must
decide within **3 months**, and may attach reasonable written conditions [bc.spa.s90.2,
bc.spr.s5.6]. Two things gate it: the request cannot be made until the electrical planning report
date has arrived [bc.spr.s5.4], and a significant change to common property still needs an owners'
vote — though only a **majority** one for EV work [bc.spa.s71].

## What the law says

**The owner's request.** An owner may request that the strata corporation approve proposed
alterations to common property, or to land that is a common asset, that are necessary to install EV
charging infrastructure for use at a parking stall [bc.spa.s90.1]. The request must contain the
prescribed information: the owner's contact details and strata lot number, a description of the
proposed infrastructure and its proposed location, the number or location of the stall, the name and
contact details of a qualified contractor, and that contractor's description of the work with
estimates of cost and time [bc.spr.s5.3].

**When it may be made** [bc.spr.s5.4]. Where the corporation must obtain an electrical planning
report, the earlier of the date it first obtains one and the date it was first required to; where it
need not obtain one, 31 December 2026. See [topic.electrical-planning-reports].

**When the corporation may approve** [bc.spa.s90.2]. It may approve if any part of the alteration
that is a significant change in the use or appearance of common property or common asset land has
been approved by the resolution the Act requires, and it is satisfied the criteria are met: the
infrastructure is in a prescribed class, and where the stall is common property or on common asset
land, the owner already has a right, permission or privilege to use that stall to the exclusion of
other owners at the time of the request. The prescribed class is EV charging infrastructure
displaying the label or mark required by section 21 of the *Electrical Safety Regulation*
[bc.spr.s5.5].

**What it may weigh** [bc.spa.s90.2]: compatibility with existing infrastructure, with infrastructure
the corporation or another owner may install, and with any system used or planned for managing the
electricity EV charging uses; and the capacity of, and current and anticipated demands on, the
electrical system. That last factor is what the electrical planning report answers
[bc.spa.s94.1].

**The limits on refusing.** The corporation **must not unreasonably refuse** a request
[bc.spa.s90.2], and must decide within the prescribed period after receiving it, which is
**3 months** [bc.spa.s90.2, bc.spr.s5.6].

**Conditions** [bc.spa.s90.2]. It may require the owner to agree in writing to reasonable
conditions, including approval of the infrastructure, contractors or materials; modifying or
replacing the infrastructure if the corporation later installs its own for the benefit of owners;
and, where more than one owner requests, joint and several liability for the expenses.

**After approval** [bc.spa.s90.3]. The corporation may make the alteration itself or let the owner
make it. The owner is responsible for the expenses unless otherwise agreed, and where the
corporation does the work the owner pays in advance an amount the corporation considers sufficient,
topping it up on request if it falls short.

**The vote.** A significant change in the use or appearance of common property or common asset land
needs a resolution at a general meeting: a **majority** vote where the change relates to installing
EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, against a 3/4 vote for any other
significant change [bc.spa.s71]. Spending from the contingency reserve fund on installing EV
charging infrastructure, or on managing the electricity it uses, likewise needs only a majority vote
[bc.spa.s96].

**The stall.** Exclusive use of common property that is not limited common property normally runs
one year at a time [bc.spa.s76]. A stall where EV charging infrastructure was installed following an
owner's request under section 90.1 is a prescribed class, and the period for it is **5 years**
[bc.spr.s5.101].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw mentions EV charging. Standard bylaw 6 requires an owner to get written approval
before altering common property, limited common property or common assets, and lets the corporation
require the owner to take responsibility for the expenses [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6]. The statutory route
in sections 90.1 to 90.3 runs alongside it and carries duties the bylaw does not: the 3 month
deadline and the bar on unreasonable refusal.

## Common mistakes

- Treating an EV request as an ordinary alteration request under the bylaws. It carries a 3 month
  decision deadline and a bar on unreasonable refusal that the bylaw does not
  [bc.spa.s90.2, bc.spr.s5.6].
- Letting the 3 months run out. The Act requires a decision within the prescribed period, not
  merely consideration [bc.spa.s90.2].
- Refusing on capacity grounds without the evidence. Capacity and anticipated demand are things the
  corporation may weigh, and the electrical planning report is where they are established
  [bc.spa.s90.2, bc.spa.s94.1].
- Putting an EV-related significant change to a 3/4 vote when a majority vote is what the Act
  requires [bc.spa.s71].
- Accepting a request before the electrical planning report gate opens [bc.spr.s5.4].
- Approving infrastructure outside the prescribed class, which is infrastructure displaying the
  label or mark the *Electrical Safety Regulation* requires [bc.spr.s5.5].
- Granting the stall for one year when a stall with charging installed on a section 90.1 request
  takes a 5 year period [bc.spa.s76, bc.spr.s5.101].
- Forgetting that where the stall is common property, the owner must already have exclusive use of
  it at the time of the request [bc.spa.s90.2].
