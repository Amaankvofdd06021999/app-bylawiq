# Strata records and who may see them

## Summary

The Act lists the records a strata corporation must prepare and the copies it must retain [bc.spa.s35], and requires it to make those records available and provide copies on request from an owner, from a tenant with assigned rights, from a person authorised in writing, and from former owners and tenants for their period — within two weeks, or one week for the bylaws and rules [bc.spa.s36]. Retention periods are in the Regulation [bc.spr.s4.1]. The list in the Act is the boundary: a record outside it is not covered by the right of access.

## What the law says

**What must be prepared.** Minutes of annual and special general meetings and of council meetings including the results of votes; a list of council members; a list of owners with their addresses, strata lot numbers, parking and storage locker numbers and unit entitlements, of mortgagees who filed a request for notification, of tenants, and of landlord-to-tenant assignments; books of account; and anything else the regulations require [bc.spa.s35]. The Regulation adds a phone number or other way to reach each council member at short notice [bc.spr.s4.1].

**What must be retained.** Copies of those records, plus the registered strata plan and amendments, the Act and regulations, the bylaws and rules, resolutions dealing with changes to common property including designation of limited common property, waivers and consents, written contracts, decisions of an arbitrator, judge or the Civil Resolution Tribunal and legal opinions, budgets and financial statements, income tax returns, correspondence, bank statements and cancelled cheques, Information Certificates, owner developer documents, depreciation reports, major-item repair reports, electrical planning reports and EV charging infrastructure reports [bc.spa.s35].

**Access.** On request the corporation must make the section 35 records available for inspection by, and provide copies to, an owner, a tenant who has been assigned the landlord's right to inspect and obtain copies, or a person authorised in writing by either [bc.spa.s36, bc.spa.s147, bc.spa.s148]. A former owner or former tenant, or someone they authorise in writing, may get records relating to the period when they were an owner or tenant, whenever those records were created [bc.spa.s36]. A tenant may always get the bylaws and rules [bc.spa.s36]. The corporation must comply within two weeks, or within one week for bylaws or rules [bc.spa.s36].

**Retention.** Permanently for the strata plan, common property resolutions, decisions and legal opinions, depreciation reports, electrical planning reports and EV reports; until disposal or replacement for major-item reports; at least 6 years for minutes, books of account, waivers and consents, budgets and financial statements, tax returns, bank records and Information Certificates, and for contracts and insurance policies from when they end; at least 2 years for correspondence; and current copies only for the council list, the owner and tenant list, the Act and regulations, the bylaws and rules, and council contact details [bc.spr.s4.1].

**Privacy law is not in the kb.** The *Personal Information Protection Act* has not been imported, so BylawIQ cannot answer what a corporation may collect, use or disclose beyond what the Strata Property Act requires. That is open question 29, and it also bears on counsel question 13.

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 19 requires council to inform owners of the minutes of every council meeting within two weeks of the meeting, whether or not they have been approved [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19]. That duty runs on its own timetable and is not answered by waiting for a records request. Separately, an Information Certificate must be given within one week of a request by an owner, a purchaser or someone they authorise, and discloses a specific list about one strata lot [bc.spa.s59]. ## Common mistakes

- Asking why an owner wants a record. The Act does not make a reason a condition of access [bc.spa.s36].
- Missing the one-week deadline for bylaws and rules by treating them as ordinary records [bc.spa.s36].
- Refusing a former owner records from their own period of ownership [bc.spa.s36].
- Treating a tenant as having no rights, without checking for an assignment [bc.spa.s147].
- Producing records that are not on the Act's list, or refusing ones that are: the list works in both directions [bc.spa.s35].
- Destroying records before the Regulation's retention period ends [bc.spr.s4.1].
- Holding council minutes back until they are approved [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19].
