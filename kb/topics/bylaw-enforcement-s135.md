# Enforcing a bylaw: the section 135 process

## Summary

Section 135 is the procedural gate in front of three penalties: a fine, the costs of remedying a contravention, and denial of a recreational facility. The corporation must have a complaint, must give the person written particulars of it and a reasonable opportunity to answer including a hearing if asked, must notify a tenant's landlord and owner, and must give its decision in writing as soon as feasible [bc.spa.s135]. Get the order wrong and the penalty is exposed, whatever the merits.

## What the law says

**When it applies.** Before imposing a fine against a person, requiring a person to pay the costs of remedying a contravention, or denying a person the use of a recreational facility, for a contravention of a bylaw or rule, the corporation must have received a complaint about the contravention, given the owner or tenant the particulars of the complaint in writing and a reasonable opportunity to answer it, including a hearing if requested, and, if the person is a tenant, given notice of the complaint to the landlord and the owner [bc.spa.s135]. Those three penalties are the enforcement options in [bc.spa.s129, bc.spa.s130, bc.spa.s133, bc.spa.s134].

**The decision.** The corporation must give written notice of its decision, as soon as is feasible, to the person and to any landlord and owner who had to be notified [bc.spa.s135].

**The hearing.** A hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2]. An owner or tenant can also request a council hearing independently, by written application stating the reason, which council must hold within four weeks [bc.spa.s34.1]. The part of a council meeting dealing with a bylaw contravention hearing is closed to owners attending as observers [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17].

**Once, not every time.** Once the corporation has complied with section 135 for a contravention of a bylaw or rule, it may fine or otherwise penalise a continuing contravention of that same bylaw or rule without complying again [bc.spa.s135].

**Who acts.** The powers and duties of the corporation are exercised by council [bc.spa.s26], and the decisions whether a person contravened, whether to fine and how much, and whether to deny a recreational facility cannot be delegated [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]. A complaint against a council member has its own provision [bc.spa.s136].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 20 is the one to read alongside section 135: council may delegate its powers and duties, including to a strata manager, but not the three fact-specific decisions above [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]. Standard bylaw 17 keeps observers out of the part of a council meeting dealing with a bylaw contravention hearing [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17], and standard bylaw 19 still requires council to inform owners of the minutes within two weeks [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19], so how a closed portion is recorded matters. ## Common mistakes

- Imposing the penalty first and inviting a response afterwards [bc.spa.s135].
- Giving the substance of a complaint by telephone or in a summary that does not let the person know what they are answering. The particulars must be in writing [bc.spa.s135].
- Refusing a hearing, or offering a written exchange in place of one [bc.spr.s7.2].
- Fining a tenant without notifying the landlord and the owner [bc.spa.s135].
- Never sending the written decision [bc.spa.s135].
- Letting the manager decide [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].
- Relying on the continuing contravention shortcut for a different bylaw from the one the process was completed for [bc.spa.s135].
- Treating the shortcut as covering a fresh contravention after a gap, when the wording is about a contravention that continues [bc.spa.s135].
