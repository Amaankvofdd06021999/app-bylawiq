# Noise in BC strata buildings

## Summary

The standard test is not silence but reasonableness: the standard bylaw forbids unreasonable noise and unreasonable interference with other people's use and enjoyment [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Buildings add quiet hours and flooring requirements on top of that. The distinction that decides most questions is bylaw versus rule: a rule can only govern common property and common assets, so it cannot regulate noise made inside a strata lot [bc.spa.s125].

## What the law says

There is no noise provision in the Act. Noise is governed by the building's bylaws, made under the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119], and by the standard bylaw where the building has filed nothing else [bc.spa.s120].

Rules are the weaker instrument, and the difference matters here. A strata corporation may make rules governing the use, safety and condition of the common property and common assets — not the inside of a strata lot. A rule is unenforceable to the same extent a bylaw is, a bylaw prevails over a conflicting rule, rules must be written down in a form capable of being photocopied, owners and tenants must be told of new rules as soon as feasible, and a new rule ceases to have effect at the first annual general meeting after it is made unless ratified by majority vote [bc.spa.s125].

A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. Bylaw amendments need a 3/4 vote and filing in the land title office to take effect [bc.spa.s128]. Enforcement runs through the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135].

Flooring is where noise and alterations meet: replacing a floor covering can be an alteration that needs written approval under the building's alteration bylaw [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 3 (1) forbids using a strata lot, the common property or the common assets in a way that causes a nuisance or hazard to another person, causes unreasonable noise, unreasonably interferes with the rights of others to use and enjoy the common property, common assets or another strata lot, is illegal, or is contrary to the purpose the strata plan shows for the lot or the common property [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Each of those is a separate ground, and a noise complaint often engages more than one. ## Common mistakes

- Putting a noise standard for the inside of a strata lot in a rule. Rules reach only the common property and common assets [bc.spa.s125].
- Relying on a rule that was never ratified at the first annual general meeting after it was made [bc.spa.s125].
- Treating quiet hours as the whole test, so noise outside those hours is ignored even where it is unreasonable [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].
- Treating any audible noise as a contravention. The standard bylaw's test is unreasonableness [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].
- Enforcing on a single complaint without written particulars and a reasonable opportunity to answer [bc.spa.s135].
- Overlooking that a noise complaint connected to a disability engages the limit in [bc.spa.s121].
