# Strata insurance and deductibles

## Summary

The corporation must insure the common property, common assets, the buildings shown on the strata plan and owner-developer-installed fixtures, generally at full replacement value [bc.spa.s149]. Owners, tenants and normal occupants are named insureds whatever the policy says [bc.spa.s155]. The deductible is a common expense unless the owner is responsible for the loss, in which case the corporation may sue to recover it [bc.spa.s158].

## What the law says

**What must be insured.** Property insurance on common property, common assets, the buildings shown on the strata plan, and fixtures built or installed on a strata lot by the owner developer as part of the original construction — the last not applying to a bare land strata plan — generally on a full replacement value basis, against the perils the Act and regulations require [bc.spa.s149]. "Major perils" is defined in the Regulation [bc.spr.s9.1]. Optional insurance for other perils and other fixtures is permitted [bc.spa.s152].

**Who is insured.** Despite the terms of the policy, named insureds include the corporation, the owners and tenants from time to time, and the persons who normally occupy the strata lots [bc.spa.s155]. An owner may separately insure their lot and fixtures, improvements, loss of rental value and liability [bc.spa.s161].

**Review and reporting.** The corporation must review the adequacy of its insurance annually, report on coverage at each annual general meeting, and inform owners and tenants as soon as feasible of a material change, including an increase in a deductible [bc.spa.s154].

**The deductible.** Paying a deductible on a claim on the corporation's insurance is a common expense contributed to through strata fees. That does not limit the corporation's capacity to sue an owner to recover it where the owner is responsible for the loss or damage. And approval is not required for a special levy or reserve fund expenditure to cover a deductible the corporation must pay to repair or replace damaged property, unless it has decided not to repair [bc.spa.s158].

**Deciding not to repair.** By 3/4 vote at a meeting held no later than 60 days after receipt of the insurance money, the corporation may decide not to repair or replace, in which case the money is held in trust and distributed according to each person's interest or as a court orders [bc.spa.s159].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw sets insurance duties; several standard bylaws reference the insurance obligation, including the use bylaw's prohibition on damage to what the corporation must insure and the entry and repair bylaws [bc.spa.s149].

## Common mistakes

- Charging a deductible to an owner as though the Act imposed it. It is a common expense unless responsibility is established [bc.spa.s158].
- Overlooking that owners, tenants and occupants are named insureds [bc.spa.s155].
- Holding a vote to fund a deductible when no approval is needed [bc.spa.s158].
- Not telling owners of a deductible increase, which the Act requires as soon as feasible [bc.spa.s154].
- Missing the 60 day window for a decision not to repair [bc.spa.s159].
