# Taking a strata dispute to the Civil Resolution Tribunal

## Summary

An owner or tenant generally cannot take a strata dispute to the tribunal until they have requested a council hearing under section 34.1, unless the tribunal directs otherwise [bc.spa.s189.1]. That single requirement is the most commonly missed step. The court route for significant unfairness is section 164, and other court orders are under section 165 [bc.spa.s164, bc.spa.s165].

## What the law says

**The gate.** A strata corporation, owner or tenant may ask the tribunal to resolve a dispute concerning any strata property matter over which it has jurisdiction. But an owner or tenant may not make that request unless they requested a council hearing under section 34.1, or the tribunal directs that the requirement does not apply [bc.spa.s189.1, bc.spa.s34.1]. The Civil Resolution Tribunal Act itself is not in the kb (task A4), so the tribunal's own jurisdiction, procedure and remedies are not set out here.

**Before collecting money.** Before suing, beginning arbitration, or a tribunal initiating notice being given in respect of collecting money from an owner or tenant, the corporation must give at least two weeks' written notice demanding payment [bc.spa.s112]. A disputed amount can be paid into court or to the corporation in trust once proceedings have started or an initiating notice has been given [bc.spa.s114].

**Court remedies.** On application of an owner or tenant, the Supreme Court may make any interim or final order it considers necessary to prevent or remedy a significantly unfair action, threatened action or decision of the corporation including council, or a significantly unfair exercise of voting rights by a person holding 50% or more of the votes; and may direct or prohibit an act, vary a transaction or resolution, or regulate the conduct of the corporation's future affairs [bc.spa.s164]. On application of an owner, tenant, mortgagee or interested person the court may order the corporation to perform a duty, or to stop contravening the Act, regulations, bylaws or rules [bc.spa.s165].

**Voluntary dispute resolution** under the bylaws does not affect anyone's right to sue, to begin arbitration or mediation, or to ask the tribunal to resolve a claim [bc.spa.s124].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 29 provides a voluntary dispute resolution committee where all parties consent and the dispute involves the Act, regulations, bylaws or rules. It is voluntary, and cannot be made a precondition to a tribunal claim [bc.spa.s124].

## Common mistakes

- Filing at the tribunal without having requested a council hearing, or a direction that the requirement does not apply [bc.spa.s189.1].
- Treating the bylaw dispute resolution committee as a required first step [bc.spa.s124].
- Starting collection proceedings without the two weeks' written demand [bc.spa.s112].
- Refusing a disputed amount paid in trust, or spending it [bc.spa.s114].
- Confusing the court's significant unfairness jurisdiction with the tribunal's own remedial power; the kb does not hold the tribunal's statute [bc.spa.s164].
