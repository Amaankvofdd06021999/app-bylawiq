# Using the common property

## Summary

Three provisions do most of the work: a significant change in the use or appearance of common property needs an owners' vote [bc.spa.s71]; exclusive use of common property can be granted for only a year at a time [bc.spa.s76]; and charging for the use of common property is a user fee, permitted only on the Regulation's terms [bc.spa.s110, bc.spr.s6.9].

## What the law says

**Significant change.** Subject to the regulations, the corporation must not make a significant change in the use or appearance of common property or land that is a common asset unless there are reasonable grounds to believe immediate change is necessary to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage, or the change is approved by resolution at a general meeting — by majority vote where it relates to installing EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, and by 3/4 vote otherwise [bc.spa.s71].

**Exclusive use.** Subject to that, the corporation may give an owner or tenant permission to exclusively use, or a special privilege in relation to, common assets or common property that is not limited common property: conditional, for no more than one year, renewable on changed terms, cancellable on reasonable notice [bc.spa.s76]. The prescribed exception is a stall with EV charging infrastructure installed on an owner's request, where the period is 5 years [bc.spr.s5.101].

**Access.** An owner with the right to use common property, including limited common property, or common assets must allow the corporation reasonable access to exercise its powers and perform its duties [bc.spa.s77].

**User fees.** Not permitted except as the Regulation allows: a reasonable amount, set out in a bylaw or a ratified rule, fixed or on a reasonable basis [bc.spa.s110, bc.spr.s6.9].

**Rules.** Rules may govern the use, safety and condition of common property and common assets — this is where rules properly operate [bc.spa.s125].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 3 limits how the common property and common assets may be used, including the nuisance, noise and interference limbs and the prohibition on damage [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Standard bylaw 6 requires written approval before an owner alters common property, limited common property or common assets [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6].

## Common mistakes

- Council approving a change that is significant enough to need an owners' vote [bc.spa.s71].
- Granting exclusive use indefinitely, when the Act allows a year at a time [bc.spa.s76].
- Charging an amenity fee with no bylaw or ratified rule behind it [bc.spr.s6.9].
- Using a rule to regulate conduct inside a strata lot [bc.spa.s125].
- Overlooking the owner's duty to allow the corporation reasonable access to common property they use [bc.spa.s77].
