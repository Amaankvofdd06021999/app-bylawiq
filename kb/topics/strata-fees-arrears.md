# Strata fees and collecting arrears

## Summary

Strata fees are each lot's share of the budgeted contributions to the operating fund and the contingency reserve fund [bc.spa.s99], set by the budget approved at the annual general meeting [bc.spa.s103]. Collection is a statutory sequence: two weeks' written demand before suing or registering a lien [bc.spa.s112], a Certificate of Lien [bc.spa.s116], and only then an application for sale [bc.spa.s117]. Arrears are not a bylaw contravention, so the fine process does not apply to them [bc.spa.s135].

## What the law says

**How fees are set.** Owners contribute their strata lots' shares of the total budgeted contributions to the operating fund and the contingency reserve fund, calculated under the Act and the regulations [bc.spa.s99]. The budget is prepared for approval by majority vote at each annual general meeting, distributed with the notice of that meeting and accompanied by a financial statement [bc.spa.s103]. Within two weeks after the meeting at which a budget is passed, the corporation must tell owners of any change to their strata fees [bc.spa.s106].

**Interest on late fees.** Only a bylaw can create it. A bylaw establishing a schedule for paying strata fees may set a rate of interest for late payment, not exceeding the rate in the regulations [bc.spa.s107], which is 10% per annum compounded annually [bc.spr.s6.8]. That interest is not a fine, and it forms part of the strata fees for the purposes of a Certificate of Lien [bc.spa.s107].

**Special levies.** The corporation may raise money by special levy, calculated under the ordinary formula and approved by 3/4 vote, or divided another fair way and approved by unanimous vote [bc.spa.s108].

**Collecting.** Before suing, starting arbitration, or a tribunal initiating notice being given in respect of collection, and separately before registering a lien, the corporation must give at least two weeks' written notice demanding payment and saying what may follow [bc.spa.s112]. Where the debt is disputed, the owner or tenant may pay the disputed amount into court or to the corporation to hold in trust, and the corporation then holds it in trust until the dispute is resolved [bc.spa.s114]. The corporation may register a Certificate of Lien in the land title office for unpaid strata fees, a special levy, reimbursement of the cost of work under section 85, or the lot's share of a judgment against the corporation [bc.spa.s116]. After registering a lien it may apply to the Supreme Court for an order for sale of the strata lot [bc.spa.s117]. Reasonable legal costs, land title and court registry fees and other reasonable disbursements of registering or enforcing the lien may be added to the amount owing [bc.spa.s118].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 1 does one thing: it requires an owner to pay strata fees on or before the first day of the month to which they relate [bc.spa.sched.bylaw1]. It sets no interest, so a building that has not amended it has no interest to charge [bc.spa.s107]. What a building can change is in [building.standard-bylaw.1].

## Common mistakes

- Charging interest on arrears without a bylaw that establishes a payment schedule and sets a rate [bc.spa.s107].
- Setting an interest rate above the regulated maximum, or expressing a monthly rate that exceeds it once compounded [bc.spr.s6.8].
- Treating unpaid fees as a bylaw contravention and running the fine process against them [bc.spa.s135].
- Registering a lien without the two weeks' written demand first [bc.spa.s112].
- Refusing a disputed payment into trust, or spending it [bc.spa.s114].
- Adding costs to a lien beyond those the Act allows [bc.spa.s118].
- Treating a lien as the end of the process: sale requires a court order [bc.spa.s117].
