# Harassment in a strata building

## Summary

The Act has no harassment provision. A harassment complaint is brought under the building's bylaws, usually the use bylaw's nuisance and unreasonable interference limbs [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3], and where the person complained about is a council member the Act requires that member to stand out of the decision [bc.spa.s136]. Conduct by council is measured against the standard of care instead [bc.spa.s31].

## What the law says

**The bylaw route.** Harassment of an owner, occupant, council member or the manager is dealt with under the building's bylaws, made under the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119], or under the standard use bylaw's prohibition on causing a nuisance or hazard to another person and on unreasonably interfering with others' use and enjoyment [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Enforcement is the ordinary process [bc.spa.s135].

**Complaints about a council member.** If a complaint is made about a council member contravening a bylaw or rule, that member must not participate in the decision about the complaint, unless all the owners are on council [bc.spa.s136].

**Conduct by council itself.** Each council member must act honestly and in good faith with a view to the best interests of the corporation and with the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person [bc.spa.s31], and must disclose and stand out of a conflict [bc.spa.s32]. A complaint that council has behaved improperly is measured against those duties rather than against a bylaw.

**The limit.** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]; conduct that is harassment on a protected ground engages the Code, which is not in the kb (task A5). Conduct that may be criminal is a matter for the police, not the bylaws.

**Privacy.** Council may exclude observers from a part of a meeting where their presence would unreasonably interfere with an individual's privacy [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw names harassment. Standard bylaw 3 is what a building without its own harassment bylaw relies on [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].

## Common mistakes

- Looking for a harassment section in the Act; there is none.
- Letting the council member complained about sit on the decision [bc.spa.s136].
- Treating a complaint about council's conduct as a bylaw contravention, when it is measured against the standard of care [bc.spa.s31].
- Handling a complaint that engages a protected ground without advice [bc.spa.s121].
- Discussing a complaint in open session where other owners are present [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17].
- Skipping the complaint and answer process because the conduct is directed at council [bc.spa.s135].
