# Alterations and renovations

## Summary

Two different questions get confused here. Whether an alteration needs **approval** is answered by the alteration bylaws [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5, bc.spa.sched.bylaw6]. Who is responsible for **repairing** the altered thing afterwards is answered by the repair provisions and the bylaws made under them [bc.spa.s72], and the Act limits how far that can be shifted. An alteration agreement cannot move a duty the Act does not allow to be moved.

## What the law says

**Approval.** Under the standard bylaws an owner must get written approval before altering listed parts of a strata lot — the structure or exterior of a building, things attached to the exterior, exterior doors, windows and skylights or those fronting on common property, fences, railings and similar structures enclosing a patio, balcony or yard, common property inside the lot boundaries, and the parts the corporation must insure [bc.spa.s149] — and that approval must not be unreasonably withheld, though it may be conditional on the owner taking responsibility in writing for the expenses [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5]. For common property, including limited common property, and common assets, written approval is also required, and may be made conditional in the same way, but there is no express duty not to withhold unreasonably [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6].

**Significant change.** Council cannot approve, under an alteration bylaw, something that is a significant change in the use or appearance of common property or of land that is a common asset: that needs a 3/4 vote, or a majority vote where it relates to EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses [bc.spa.s71].

**Responsibility afterwards.** The corporation must repair and maintain common property and common assets; by bylaw it may make an owner responsible for limited common property the owner may use, and for other common property only where the Regulation identifies it and subject to the prescribed restrictions; and it may take on specified portions of a strata lot [bc.spa.s72]. Taking on portions of some lots and not others changes the cost sharing [bc.spr.s6.5, bc.spa.sched.bylaw8].

**EV charging.** An owner may request approval of alterations to common property, or land that is a common asset, necessary to install EV charging infrastructure for use at a parking stall, and the corporation may approve where any significant change has been approved by the required resolution and the prescribed criteria are met [bc.spa.s90.1, bc.spa.s90.2].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 5 governs alterations to a strata lot and does not apply in a bare land strata plan; standard bylaw 6 governs alterations to common property, limited common property and common assets [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5, bc.spa.sched.bylaw6]. ## Common mistakes

- Using an alteration agreement to move a repair obligation the Act does not permit to be moved [bc.spa.s72].
- Approving at council level a change that needed a 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s71].
- Withholding approval for a strata lot alteration without a reason, where the standard bylaw requires that approval not be unreasonably withheld [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5].
- Assuming an alteration agreement binds a later owner without checking whether it says so.
- Forgetting that altering a part the corporation must insure engages the insurance duty whatever the agreement says [bc.spa.s149].
