# Human rights and accommodation in strata

## Summary

The *Human Rights Code* is not in the knowledge base (research task A5), so this guide does not state when accommodation is required or what undue hardship means. What it can state is the hook in the Strata Property Act — a bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Code [bc.spa.s121] — and the situations the Act answers outright, which are pets [bc.spa.s123] and age restrictions [bc.spa.s123.1, bc.spa.s123.2].

## What the law says

**The hook.** A bylaw is not enforceable to the extent that it contravenes this Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or any other enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. A bylaw can therefore be validly passed and filed and still be unenforceable against a particular person. That is why enforcement, not the request, is where the exposure lies.

**Pets, answered by the Act.** A bylaw prohibiting a pet does not apply to a pet already living there lawfully before the bylaw passed, while it goes on living there; and a bylaw prohibiting or restricting an animal, or its access, never applies to a guide dog, a service dog, or a dog of a retired guide or service dog team whose member is an owner, tenant or occupant [bc.spa.s123]. Those need no case-by-case analysis. The definitions come from the *Guide Dog and Service Dog Act*, which the kb does not hold (open question 32).

**Age, answered by the Act.** A bylaw must not restrict the age of persons who may reside in a strata lot, except that the corporation may require one or more residents to have reached a specified age of not less than 55 [bc.spa.s123.1]. Such a requirement does not apply to a person who was already residing there lawfully before the bylaw passed and continues to, to a caregiver residing there to care for a dependent resident, or to a prescribed class [bc.spa.s123.2]. The prescribed classes are a child one of whose caregivers is a specified resident, a person 19 or over residing with a specified resident who was their caregiver before they turned 19, and the spouse of a specified resident [bc.spr.s7.01].

**Everything else** needs the Code, and advice. The process is in [firm.policy.accommodation-request].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw addresses accommodation. Standard bylaw 3's pet allowance is what a building without its own pet bylaw has, and the statutory exemptions apply to any pet bylaw regardless [bc.spa.s123].

## Common mistakes

- Asking a person with a guide dog or service dog to prove a disability. The exemption turns on what the dog is [bc.spa.s123].
- Running an accommodation analysis on a grandfathered pet, which the Act has already answered [bc.spa.s123].
- Applying an age restriction to a caregiver, a child of a specified resident, or a spouse [bc.spa.s123.2, bc.spr.s7.01].
- Passing an age bylaw below 55 [bc.spa.s123.1].
- Continuing to enforce while a request is unresolved [bc.spa.s135].
- Deciding undue hardship without advice; the test is not in the kb.
