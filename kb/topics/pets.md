# Pets in BC strata buildings

## Summary

A building may restrict pets, but two statutory limits sit above whatever the bylaw says: a pet that was already living there lawfully when the bylaw passed is not caught by it, and a guide dog or service dog is never caught by it [bc.spa.s123]. Where a building has filed no pet bylaw of its own, the standard bylaw's allowance applies [bc.spa.s120, bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Enforcement is the ordinary bylaw enforcement process, and a fine imposed without it is exposed however clear the contravention [bc.spa.s135].

## What the law says

The bylaw power is general: bylaws may provide for the control, management, maintenance, use and enjoyment of the strata lots, common property and common assets [bc.spa.s119]. Pet bylaws sit inside two express limits.

First, grandfathering. A bylaw that prohibits a pet does not apply to a pet living with an owner, tenant or occupant if, immediately before the bylaw was passed, the pet lived with them and doing so contravened no pet bylaw, and the pet goes on living with them [bc.spa.s123]. The protection attaches to that pet and lasts while it keeps living there.

Second, assistance animals. A bylaw that prohibits a pet or other animal, or restricts its access to a strata lot or common property, does not apply to a guide dog or service dog, or to a dog that is a member of a retired guide or service dog team where the team member is an owner, tenant or occupant [bc.spa.s123]. Those three terms take their meaning from the *Guide Dog and Service Dog Act*, which the kb does not hold: see open question 32.

Beyond those, a bylaw is unenforceable to the extent that it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. That is the route by which a request about an animal that is not a guide or service dog is assessed, and the kb cannot yet cite the Code itself (task A5).

A pet bylaw only becomes a bylaw of the building once it is passed by 3/4 vote and the Amendment to Bylaws is filed in the land title office; until filing it has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. Fines for contravening it are capped by the building's own bylaws and by the Regulation [bc.spa.s130, bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 3 is both the pet bylaw and the nuisance bylaw. It requires animals to be leashed or otherwise secured on common property or on land that is a common asset, and it allows a resident to keep a reasonable number of fish or other small aquarium animals, a reasonable number of small caged mammals, up to two caged birds, and one dog or one cat [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. It applies unless the building has filed a different bylaw on the subject [bc.spa.s120]. What a building can change, and what to look for in its registered bylaws, is in [building.standard-bylaw.3]; the shapes a pet bylaw takes are in [building.bylaw-pattern.pets].

## Common mistakes

- Applying a new limit to a pet that was living there lawfully before the bylaw passed. The Act's protection operates whatever the bylaw says [bc.spa.s123].
- Treating grandfathering as a right attached to the home or the resident. It attaches to the pet, for as long as that pet goes on living there [bc.spa.s123].
- Asking a resident with a guide dog or service dog to prove a disability. The exemption turns on what the dog is, not on the handler's condition [bc.spa.s123].
- Enforcing a pet bylaw that was passed but never filed [bc.spa.s128].
- Fining before giving written particulars of the complaint and a reasonable opportunity to answer, including a hearing if one is requested [bc.spa.s135].
- Fining above the maximum in the building's own bylaws, which is $50 for a bylaw contravention where the standard bylaws still apply [bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1].
