# Electrical planning reports

## Summary

A strata corporation must obtain an electrical planning report from a qualified person by dates the
Regulation sets [bc.spa.s94.1]. For a strata corporation that already existed at the end of 2023,
those dates are **31 December 2026** if any of its land is in the Capital Regional District, the
Fraser Valley Regional District or the Metro Vancouver Regional District, and **31 December 2028**
otherwise [bc.spr.s5.8, bc.spr.s5.7]. A strata plan with fewer than 5 strata lots is exempt
[bc.spr.s5.8]. This is a different report from a depreciation report, with its own deadline, its own
qualified person and its own contents.

## What the law says

**The duty.** Subject to the regulations, a strata corporation must obtain from a qualified person,
on or before the dates determined in accordance with the regulations, an electrical planning report
containing the information the regulations set out [bc.spa.s94.1].

**Who counts as existing, and where.** An "existing strata corporation" is one established on or
before 31 December 2023; a "new strata corporation" is one established after that date. A
"specified area" is the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District or the Metro
Vancouver Regional District, in the first and third cases excluding an island reachable only by air
or boat [bc.spr.s5.7].

**Deadlines, strata plan that is not phased** [bc.spr.s5.8]:

- An existing strata corporation: on or before **31 December 2026** if the land in the strata plan
  is wholly or partly in a specified area, and **31 December 2028** if it is wholly outside one.
- It need not obtain one at all if the strata plan had fewer than 5 strata lots on
  31 December 2023.
- A new strata corporation: on or before the date 5 years after the strata plan was deposited, and
  not at all if the plan had fewer than 5 strata lots at deposit.

**Deadlines, phased strata plan** [bc.spr.s5.9]: an existing strata corporation whose phases
deposited as of 31 December 2023 include at least one phase with 5 or more strata lots is on the
same two dates, by reference to where the deposited phases are. On top of that, each time a phase of
5 or more strata lots is deposited after 31 December 2023, a report is due 5 years after that
phase's deposit, and the same 5 year rule runs per qualifying phase for a new strata corporation.
Where every phase has fewer than 5 strata lots, the corporation may defer until 5 years after the
final phase is deposited. A phased corporation's report need only cover the phases the Regulation
lists [bc.spr.s5.12].

**Who may prepare it** [bc.spr.s5.10]. Where every building in the strata plan is a Part 9 building
under the British Columbia Building Code, a qualified person is a professional engineer or
professional licensee engineering registered with Engineers and Geoscientists BC, an applied science
technologist or certified technician registered with ASTTBC, **or** a journeyperson in the
construction electrician or industrial electrician trade. For any other strata corporation only the
first two qualify: a journeyperson is not enough.

**What it must contain** [bc.spr.s5.11]. The date; the provider's name, qualifications, error and
omission insurance if any, and relationship to the corporation; the current capacity of the
electrical system; a list of existing demands on it, including EV charging infrastructure and
heating, cooling, ventilation and lighting; the current peak demand and spare capacity; where
applicable an estimate of the capacity that would be needed to power systems currently running on
another energy source; and an estimate of the capacity needed for other anticipated future demands.

**Paying for it.** The cost of obtaining an electrical planning report is a purpose of the operating
fund [bc.spa.s92], and an expenditure from the contingency reserve fund that is necessary to obtain
one may be approved by a **majority** vote rather than a 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s96].

**Keeping it.** Any electrical planning report the corporation obtains is a record it must retain
[bc.spa.s35], permanently [bc.spr.s4.1], and it must be produced on a records request
[bc.spa.s36].

**Why the date matters beyond compliance.** An owner cannot ask to install EV charging
infrastructure on common property until the corporation has obtained its first electrical planning
report, or until the date that report was due, whichever is earlier; where no report is required at
all, the date is 31 December 2026 [bc.spr.s5.4]. The report is the gate on the EV request process
in [topic.ev-charging].

## What the standard bylaws say

Nothing. No standard bylaw deals with electrical planning reports; the duty is statutory and applies
whatever the building's bylaws say.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing it with the depreciation report. They have different deadlines, different qualified
  persons and different contents [bc.spa.s94.1, topic.depreciation-report].
- Reading the 31 December 2026 date as applying province-wide. It applies to a strata corporation
  whose land is wholly or partly in one of the three named regional districts; everywhere else the
  date is 31 December 2028 [bc.spr.s5.8, bc.spr.s5.7].
- Missing the island exception inside the Capital Regional District and Metro Vancouver, which takes
  an island reachable only by air or boat out of the specified area [bc.spr.s5.7].
- Testing the fewer-than-5-lots exemption against today's lot count rather than the count on
  31 December 2023, or at deposit for a new corporation [bc.spr.s5.8].
- Engaging a journeyperson electrician for a building that is not a Part 9 building, where only an
  engineer or an applied science technologist qualifies [bc.spr.s5.10].
- Treating a phased strata plan as having one deadline. It can have several, one per qualifying
  phase [bc.spr.s5.9].
- Putting the expenditure to a 3/4 vote when a majority vote is enough to fund obtaining the report
  from the contingency reserve fund [bc.spa.s96].
- Telling an owner they may request EV charging before the report gate opens [bc.spr.s5.4].
