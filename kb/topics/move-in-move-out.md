# Move-in and move-out fees and procedures

## Summary

A move fee is a user fee for the use of common property, and the Act is restrictive about those: the corporation must not impose one except as the Regulation allows, which means the amount must be reasonable and it must be set out in a bylaw, or in a rule that has been ratified [bc.spa.s110, bc.spr.s6.9]. A procedure is easier to impose than a charge, and the two should not be confused.

## What the law says

**The fee.** A strata corporation must not impose user fees for the use of common property or common assets by owners, tenants or occupants or their visitors other than as set out in the regulations [bc.spa.s110]. The Regulation permits a fee only if the amount is reasonable and the fee is set out in a bylaw, or in a rule ratified at a general meeting; it may be a fixed amount or set on a reasonable basis such as consumption, recovery of operating or maintenance costs, the number of users or the duration of use [bc.spr.s6.9]. An unratified rule cannot support a fee [bc.spa.s125].

**The procedure.** Booking an elevator, protecting common areas, permitted hours and making good damage rest on the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119], and a rule can carry them so far as they concern common property [bc.spa.s125].

**Three different kinds of money.** A user fee is not a fine, and a fine is capped by the bylaws within the regulated maximum [bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1]; a charge for actual damage is neither. Where a charge is in truth a penalty for a contravention, the complaint and answer process applies [bc.spa.s135].

**The rental limit.** A move-in procedure that in substance screens or approves tenants, or requires terms in a tenancy agreement, runs into the prohibition on restricting rentals [bc.spa.s141, bc.spa.s121].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw deals with moving. A building with only the standard bylaws has no move fee at all, because there is no bylaw or ratified rule to support one [bc.spr.s6.9]. The bylaw shapes are in [building.bylaw-pattern.move-in-move-out].

## Common mistakes

- Charging a move fee under an unratified rule [bc.spa.s125, bc.spr.s6.9].
- Setting a fee the building cannot explain by reference to any cost or basis [bc.spr.s6.9].
- Treating a refundable damage deposit as a fine, or applying it without the enforcement process where it is in truth a penalty [bc.spa.s135].
- Using the move-in form to collect tenant approval or screening information [bc.spa.s141].
- Charging the tenant rather than the owner, where the Act's collection machinery runs against owners.
