# Amending and filing bylaws

## Summary

An amendment takes effect on **filing**, not on the vote: it must be approved at a general meeting and an Amendment to Bylaws in the prescribed form filed in the land title office, and until that filing the amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. Before the second annual general meeting, a wholly residential or bare land strata plan needs a unanimous vote to amend at all [bc.spa.s127].

## What the law says

**The power.** The corporation must have bylaws, and they may provide for the control, management, maintenance, use and enjoyment of the strata lots, common property and common assets [bc.spa.s119]. They are the Standard Bylaws except to the extent different bylaws are filed [bc.spa.s120]. They may be changed, repealed, replaced, added to or amended by complying with the Division [bc.spa.s126].

**The vote.** Approval at an annual or special general meeting: by 3/4 vote for a wholly residential plan; by 3/4 vote or as otherwise provided in the bylaws for a wholly nonresidential plan; and for a mixed plan, by both a 3/4 vote of the residential lots and a 3/4 vote of the nonresidential lots, or as the bylaws otherwise provide for the nonresidential lots [bc.spa.s128]. Sections have their own rule [bc.spa.s197].

**Before the second annual general meeting.** For a bare land strata plan, or where all lots are residential, no amendment may be made before the second annual general meeting unless approved by unanimous vote; where all lots are nonresidential the ordinary route applies [bc.spa.s127].

**Filing.** An Amendment to Bylaws setting out the amendment, in the prescribed form, must be filed in the land title office, and until filing the amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. Owners and tenants must be told of an amendment as soon as feasible after approval [bc.spa.s128], and unfiled amendments are among the things an Information Certificate discloses [bc.spa.s59].

**Limits.** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. Rules are a different instrument with their own rules [bc.spa.s125].

## What the standard bylaws say

The standard bylaws apply to the extent nothing different is filed, which is why the filed set and every amendment are what matter [bc.spa.s120].

## Common mistakes

- Enforcing an amendment that was passed but never filed [bc.spa.s128].
- Amending before the second annual general meeting on a 3/4 vote, in a residential or bare land plan [bc.spa.s127].
- Forgetting the separate nonresidential vote in a mixed plan [bc.spa.s128].
- Adopting a "policy" that is not a bylaw and enforcing it as one [bc.spa.s125].
- Leaving an unenforceable bylaw on file because it is easier than repealing it [bc.spa.s121].
- Not disclosing unfiled amendments on an Information Certificate [bc.spa.s59].
