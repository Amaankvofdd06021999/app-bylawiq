# Renting out a strata lot in BC

## Summary

A strata corporation cannot restrict the rental of a strata lot. It must not screen tenants, set screening criteria, require approval of tenants, require terms to be put in tenancy agreements, or otherwise restrict renting [bc.spa.s141]. Bylaws that do any of that are unenforceable, whether or not they are still filed [bc.spa.s121]. What survives is the information side: a landlord's duties to the tenant and to the corporation [bc.spa.s146], the assignment of a landlord's rights to a tenant [bc.spa.s147], and the landlord's continuing responsibility for the tenant's fines [bc.spa.s131].

## What the law says

**No rental restrictions.** Section 141 is short and absolute in its terms: the strata corporation must not screen tenants, establish screening criteria, require the approval of tenants, require the insertion of terms in tenancy agreements or otherwise restrict the rental of a strata lot [bc.spa.s141]. The sections that used to support rental restriction bylaws, ss. 142 to 145, were repealed in 2022 and are not in the kb. A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act [bc.spa.s121], so an old rental restriction bylaw is unenforceable even though it remains on file; filing is not what makes a bylaw good [bc.spa.s128].

**What a landlord must do.** Before renting all or part of a residential strata lot, the landlord must give the prospective tenant the current bylaws and rules and a Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities in the prescribed form, which is Form K of the Schedule of Forms [bc.spr.form.k], and within two weeks of renting must give the strata corporation a copy of the notice signed by the tenant. If the landlord does not, the tenant is still bound by the bylaws and rules but may end the tenancy without penalty within 90 days of learning of the failure, and the landlord must then pay the tenant's reasonable moving expenses up to one month's rent [bc.spa.s146].

**Assigning a landlord's rights.** A landlord may assign some or all of the powers and duties arising under the Act, the bylaws or the rules to a tenant, but may not assign the responsibility for fines or the costs of remedying a contravention. The assignment takes effect only when the corporation has written notice naming the tenant, the powers and duties assigned and the period [bc.spa.s147, bc.spa.s148].

**Fines against a tenant.** The corporation may collect a fine or the costs of remedying a contravention from the tenant, the tenant's landlord and the owner, but not more in total than the fine or costs; a landlord or owner who pays is owed that amount by the tenant [bc.spa.s131]. Where the person being fined is a tenant, the complaint process requires notice to the landlord and the owner as well [bc.spa.s135].

**Ending a tenancy.** A repeated or continuing contravention of a reasonable and significant bylaw or rule lets the landlord give notice ending the tenancy under the *Residential Tenancy Act* [bc.spa.s137]; where the contravention also seriously interferes with another person's use and enjoyment of a strata lot, the common property or the common assets, the strata corporation itself may give that notice [bc.spa.s138]. The *Residential Tenancy Act* is not in the kb yet (task A6).

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw deals with renting. The standard bylaws apply to a tenant in the same way as to an owner, because they govern the use of the strata lots and common property [bc.spa.s119]. The bylaw shapes to look for, and the ones that no longer work, are in [building.bylaw-pattern.rentals].

## Common mistakes

- Enforcing a rental restriction bylaw that is still on file [bc.spa.s141, bc.spa.s121].
- Treating a pre-2022 tenant approval or screening step as still valid [bc.spa.s141].
- Requiring particular terms in a tenancy agreement [bc.spa.s141].
- Confusing a restriction on renting, which is barred, with a requirement to give information about a tenancy, which the Act itself imposes [bc.spa.s146].
- Treating a tenant as unable to vote or to stand for council without checking whether the landlord has filed an assignment [bc.spa.s147].
- Fining a tenant without notifying the landlord and the owner [bc.spa.s135].
- Collecting the same fine twice, from the tenant and again from the owner [bc.spa.s131].
