# Short-term rentals in BC strata buildings

## Summary

The Regulation assumes a building can have a bylaw prohibiting or limiting the use of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation, because it sets a higher maximum fine of $1,000 and allows daily fines for contravening one [bc.spr.s7.1]. But those are ceilings the building's own bylaws have to claim [bc.spa.s132], and a building that has never amended the standard bylaws is limited to $50 and one fine a week [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spa.sched.bylaw24]. How this fits with the bar on restricting rental [bc.spa.s141] is an open question, not something this guide settles.

## What the law says

**The fine provisions.** For a bylaw that prohibits or limits use of all or part of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation, the maximum fine is $1,000 for each contravention, against $200 for an ordinary bylaw contravention and $50 for a rule; and a fine may be imposed daily for a continuing contravention, against every 7 days otherwise [bc.spr.s7.1]. The corporation must set its own maximum in its bylaws and may set different maximums for different bylaws, but cannot exceed the regulated figures [bc.spa.s132].

**The authority for the bylaw itself.** There is no section of the Act that expressly authorises a short-term accommodation bylaw. It rests on the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119], and on the standard use bylaw where the building relies on a use restriction instead [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Like every bylaw, it needs a 3/4 vote and filing [bc.spa.s128] and is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121].

**The unresolved point.** Section 141 says the corporation must not screen tenants, require approval of tenants, require terms in tenancy agreements or otherwise restrict the rental of a strata lot [bc.spa.s141]. Nothing in the Act or the Regulation as imported draws the line between a rental that cannot be restricted and short-term accommodation that the Regulation assumes can be. The kb does not answer that; it is open question 33, and it needs counsel and the decisions in phase C.

**Outside the kb.** The Act and the Regulation say nothing about the provincial short-term rental registration scheme or about municipal licensing. Neither is in the kb, so BylawIQ cannot answer about them at all.

## What the standard bylaws say

There is no standard bylaw about short-term accommodation. A building with no bylaw of its own has the standard use bylaw, which forbids use that causes a nuisance or hazard, unreasonable noise, unreasonable interference with others, use that is illegal, or use contrary to the purpose shown for the lot on the strata plan [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3] — and the standard maximum fine of $50 with one fine a week [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spa.sched.bylaw24]. ## Common mistakes

- Fining $1,000 under a bylaw that never set a maximum above the standard $50. The Regulation's figure is a ceiling, not an entitlement [bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1].
- Fining daily when the building's bylaws still allow a fine only every 7 days [bc.spa.sched.bylaw24, bc.spr.s7.1].
- Raising the maximum fine without also amending the continuing contravention bylaw [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spa.sched.bylaw24].
- Treating an advertisement as the contravention when the bylaw prohibits the accommodation. They are different bylaws and need different evidence.
- Skipping the complaint and answer process because a listing looks conclusive [bc.spa.s135].
- Assuming the Act settles whether a short-term accommodation bylaw survives section 141. It does not [bc.spa.s141].
