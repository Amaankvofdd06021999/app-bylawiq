# Smoking and cannabis in a strata building

## Summary

The Act and the Regulation say nothing about smoking, vaping, tobacco or cannabis. Everything rests on the building's own bylaws and on the standard use bylaw's nuisance, hazard and unreasonable interference limbs [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Two consequences follow: there is no statutory grandfathering for smokers, and the *Human Rights Code* limit is the live constraint on both sides of a smoke drift dispute [bc.spa.s121].

## What the law says

**No statutory basis, and no statutory exemption.** A smoking bylaw is made under the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119] and needs a 3/4 vote and filing [bc.spa.s128]. The Act's grandfathering provision is about pets and applies only to pets [bc.spa.s123], so an exemption for residents who already smoke exists only if the building's own bylaw creates one, and lasts only as long as that bylaw does.

**Where a building has no smoking bylaw.** Smoke drift is dealt with under the standard use bylaw: causing a nuisance or hazard to another person, or unreasonably interfering with another person's use and enjoyment [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. The complaint has to be framed that way, because there is no smoking contravention to allege.

**The limit.** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. That cuts both ways: for a resident who uses cannabis for a medical reason, and for a resident whose health is affected by drift. The Code is not in the kb (task A5), so this guide does not state the test.

**Enforcement** is the ordinary process [bc.spa.s135].

## What the standard bylaws say

There is no standard bylaw about smoking. Standard bylaw 3 is what a building without its own smoking bylaw has [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. The bylaw shapes, including the grandfathering problem, are in [building.bylaw-pattern.smoking-cannabis].

## Common mistakes

- Assuming existing smokers are protected the way existing pets are. They are not [bc.spa.s123].
- Writing a grandfathering clause without saying whether it runs with the person or the strata lot.
- Alleging a smoking contravention where the building has no smoking bylaw, instead of nuisance or interference [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].
- Treating a medical cannabis authorisation as the end of the analysis in either direction [bc.spa.s121].
- Skipping the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135].
