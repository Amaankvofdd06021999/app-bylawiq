# Strata council: powers, duties and meetings

## Summary

The corporation acts through council [bc.spa.s4, bc.spa.s26], and council is elected at each annual general meeting [bc.spa.s25]. Owners can direct council by majority vote, but not on the fact-specific enforcement decisions [bc.spa.s27] — which council also cannot delegate [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]. Each member owes a statutory standard of care [bc.spa.s31] and must disclose conflicts [bc.spa.s32].

## What the law says

**Powers.** The powers and duties of the corporation must be exercised and performed by council unless the Act, regulations or bylaws provide otherwise [bc.spa.s4, bc.spa.s26]. Owners may direct or restrict council by majority vote at a general meeting, but not where the direction is contrary to the Act, regulations or bylaws, or interferes with council's discretion to determine, on the facts, whether a person contravened, whether to fine and how much, or whether to deny a recreational facility [bc.spa.s27].

**Who may sit.** Owners, individuals representing corporate owners, and tenants assigned the landlord's right to stand. A bylaw passed after the first annual general meeting may allow other classes; a bylaw may bar a person in respect of a strata lot the corporation could lien [bc.spa.s28].

**Standard of care.** Each council member must act honestly and in good faith with a view to the best interests of the corporation, and exercise the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person in comparable circumstances [bc.spa.s31].

**Conflicts.** A member with a direct or indirect interest in a contract or transaction with the corporation, or in a matter council is to consider where the interest could materially conflict, must disclose the nature and extent fully and promptly, abstain from voting, and leave the meeting while it is discussed and voted on [bc.spa.s32]. Failure engages the accountability provision, unless the contract is ratified by 3/4 vote after full disclosure, with the interested person not an eligible voter [bc.spa.s33].

**Remuneration** must be approved in advance in the budget, the bylaws, or by 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s34]. **Hearings** requested by an owner or tenant must be held within four weeks [bc.spa.s34.1]. A defect in a member's election does not by itself invalidate a contract or certificate, subject to what the other party knew [bc.spa.s30].

## What the standard bylaws say

The standard bylaws set council at 3 to 7 members, with all owners on council in a building of fewer than 4 lots or owners [bc.spa.sched.bylaw9]; officers elected at the first meeting after each annual general meeting [bc.spa.sched.bylaw13]; quorum by council size [bc.spa.sched.bylaw16]; electronic meetings and observers [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17]; decisions by majority of those present with a presidential tie-break and votes recorded [bc.spa.sched.bylaw18]; minutes to owners within two weeks [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19]; limits on delegation [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]; spending restrictions [bc.spa.sched.bylaw21]; and protection from personal liability for a member acting honestly and in good faith [bc.spa.sched.bylaw22]. See [building.standard-bylaw.20].

## Common mistakes

- Letting a general meeting resolution direct council on a particular enforcement decision [bc.spa.s27].
- Delegating the decision to fine to the manager or to one member [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].
- Treating disclosure of a conflict as enough, without abstaining and leaving the meeting [bc.spa.s32].
- Paying a council member without prior approval in the budget, bylaws or by 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s34].
- Not recording votes in the council minutes [bc.spa.sched.bylaw18].
- Holding council minutes back until they are approved [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19].
