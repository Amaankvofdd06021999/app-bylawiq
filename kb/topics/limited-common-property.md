# Limited common property

## Summary

Limited common property is still common property, designated for the exclusive use of one or more strata lots [bc.spa.s73]. That single fact answers most balcony and patio questions: exclusive use does not make it the owner's to repair, alter or ignore. Designation and removal each have their own route, and a 3/4 vote designation must be filed with a sketch plan [bc.spa.s74].

## What the law says

**Designation.** By the owner developer on the strata plan when deposited or by a plan amendment; by an amendment to the strata plan; or by a resolution passed by 3/4 vote at a general meeting [bc.spa.s73]. A 3/4 vote designation must be filed in the land title office with a sketch plan that satisfies the registrar, defines the areas, and specifies each strata lot whose owners have the exclusive use [bc.spa.s74].

**Removal.** Where the designation was made by the owner developer at deposit or by plan amendment, or by an amendment to the strata plan, it can only be removed by amending the plan [bc.spa.s75].

**Repair.** The corporation must repair and maintain common property, which includes limited common property, unless a bylaw makes the owner who may use it responsible [bc.spa.s72]. Under the standard bylaws the split is by frequency and by building element [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8, bc.spa.sched.bylaw2].

**Alteration and access.** An owner must get written approval before altering limited common property [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6], a significant change in the use or appearance of common property needs a vote [bc.spa.s71], and the owner must allow the corporation reasonable access [bc.spa.s77].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 8 makes the corporation responsible for limited common property for repair and maintenance that in the ordinary course occurs less often than once a year, and for the listed building elements however often they need work — structure, exterior, things attached to the exterior, exterior doors, windows and skylights, and fences, railings and similar structures enclosing patios, balconies and yards [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8]. Standard bylaw 2 leaves the rest with the owner who uses it [bc.spa.sched.bylaw2].

## Common mistakes

- Treating limited common property as belonging to the owner. It is common property [bc.spa.s73].
- Assuming exclusive use means exclusive responsibility [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8].
- Designating by 3/4 vote and not filing the resolution with a sketch plan [bc.spa.s74].
- Trying to remove an owner-developer designation by a vote rather than a plan amendment [bc.spa.s75].
- Letting an owner enclose or alter a balcony without approval [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6].
