# Nuisance in a strata building

## Summary

Nuisance is one limb of the standard use bylaw, alongside unreasonable noise, unreasonable interference, illegal use and use contrary to the strata plan's purpose [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. It is the provision that catches odour, smoke drift, second-hand effects and behaviour that has no bylaw of its own, which is why it does so much work in buildings whose bylaws are otherwise thin.

## What the law says

The Act contains no nuisance provision. The source is the building's bylaws, made under the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119], and the standard bylaw where nothing else is filed [bc.spa.s120].

The limbs are separate and a complaint often engages more than one: causing a nuisance or hazard to another person; causing unreasonable noise; unreasonably interfering with the rights of other persons to use and enjoy the common property, common assets or another strata lot; illegal use; and use contrary to the purpose shown for the lot or common property on the strata plan [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. Naming the right limb matters, because the evidence differs.

A rule cannot carry a nuisance standard into a strata lot: rules govern only the use, safety and condition of common property and common assets [bc.spa.s125]. A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. Enforcement runs through the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 3 (1) is the nuisance bylaw, and 3 (2) adds a prohibition on causing damage beyond reasonable wear and tear to common property, common assets and the parts of a strata lot the corporation must repair or insure [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. See [building.standard-bylaw.3].

## Common mistakes

- Treating "nuisance" as covering anything a neighbour dislikes; the limb has content and needs evidence.
- Using a rule to reach conduct inside a strata lot [bc.spa.s125].
- Picking the wrong limb, so the evidence gathered does not match the allegation [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].
- Enforcing without the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135].
- Missing that the conduct may engage the *Human Rights Code* limit [bc.spa.s121].
