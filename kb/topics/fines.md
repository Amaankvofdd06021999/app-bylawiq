# Fines for bylaw and rule contraventions

## Summary

A fine is one of three enforcement options, alongside remedying the contravention and denying a recreational facility [bc.spa.s129]. Three things have to line up: the building's bylaws must set a maximum, and it cannot exceed the regulated figures [bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1]; the complaint and answer process must have been followed [bc.spa.s135]; and the decision must be council's own, because it cannot be delegated [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].

## What the law says

**The options.** To enforce a bylaw or rule the corporation may impose a fine, remedy the contravention, or deny access to a recreational facility, and may do more than one. It may also simply warn, or give time to comply [bc.spa.s129, bc.spa.s130, bc.spa.s133, bc.spa.s134].

**The maximums.** The corporation must set out in its bylaws the maximum it may fine for each contravention, may set different maximums for different bylaws and rules and a frequency for continuing contraventions, and must not exceed the regulated maximums [bc.spa.s132]. Those are $200 for each contravention of a bylaw, $50 for each contravention of a rule, and $1,000 for each contravention of a bylaw prohibiting or limiting use of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation; the frequency limit is every 7 days, and daily for that short-term accommodation bylaw [bc.spr.s7.1]. A bylaw figure above the cap is unenforceable to the extent of the excess [bc.spa.s121].

**The process.** The corporation must not impose a fine, require payment of the costs of remedying a contravention, or deny use of a recreational facility unless it has received a complaint, given the owner or tenant the particulars of the complaint in writing and a reasonable opportunity to answer it including a hearing if requested, and, where the person is a tenant, given notice of the complaint to the landlord and the owner. It must then give written notice of its decision as soon as feasible [bc.spa.s135]. A hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2]. Once the corporation has complied for a contravention, it may fine for a continuing contravention of the same bylaw or rule without doing it again [bc.spa.s135].

**Who decides, and who pays.** Council may not delegate the decisions whether a person contravened, whether to fine and how much, or whether to deny a recreational facility [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]. A fine against a tenant may be collected from the tenant, the landlord and the owner, but not more than once over [bc.spa.s131].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 23 sets the maximum at $50 for each contravention of a bylaw and $10 for each contravention of a rule, well under what the Regulation allows [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spr.s7.1]. Standard bylaw 24 allows a fine every 7 days where a contravention continues without interruption for longer than 7 days [bc.spa.sched.bylaw24]. A building that has never amended these is held to those figures, whatever the Regulation permits. The shapes a fine bylaw takes are in [building.bylaw-pattern.fines].

## Common mistakes

- Fining above the building's own maximum, or above the regulated cap [bc.spa.s132, bc.spr.s7.1].
- Fining more often than the bylaws allow [bc.spa.sched.bylaw24].
- Treating a warning letter as the written particulars and the opportunity to answer [bc.spa.s135].
- Letting the strata manager decide the fine rather than council [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].
- Not notifying the landlord and the owner when the person fined is a tenant [bc.spa.s135].
- Not giving written notice of the decision [bc.spa.s135].
- Using the continuing contravention shortcut before the process has been completed once for that contravention [bc.spa.s135].
- Offering a written response only, where a hearing was requested: a hearing means being heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2].
