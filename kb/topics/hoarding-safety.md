# Hoarding, fire safety and access to a strata lot

## Summary

Three tools, in order of escalation: the use bylaw's nuisance and hazard limbs [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3], the entry bylaw [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7], and the power to remedy a contravention and charge the reasonable costs [bc.spa.s133]. Where a public authority has issued a work order, the Act has its own sequence [bc.spa.s84, bc.spa.s85]. Hoarding often engages the *Human Rights Code* limit, so enforcement and accommodation run together [bc.spa.s121].

## What the law says

**The bylaw.** The standard use bylaw forbids use of a strata lot, common property or common assets that causes a nuisance or hazard to another person, or unreasonably interferes with others' use and enjoyment [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3]. That is the provision a hoarding or fire-load complaint is normally brought under where the building has no bylaw of its own.

**Entry.** Under the standard bylaw, entry must be allowed in an emergency without notice to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage, and at a reasonable time on 48 hours' written notice giving the date, approximate time and reason, to inspect, repair or maintain common property, common assets and the parts of a lot the corporation is responsible for [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7].

**Work orders.** Where the corporation receives a notice or order from a public or local authority requiring work on a strata lot, it must promptly pass it to the owner, who must do the work. If the owner does not, the corporation may do it; it must wait for an appeal if told of one in writing; and except in an emergency it must give a week's written notice of its intention to do the work [bc.spa.s84, bc.spa.s85]. Amounts reimbursable for that work can be secured by a Certificate of Lien.

**Remedying.** The corporation may do what is reasonably necessary to remedy a contravention, including work on a strata lot and removing objects from common property, and may require the reasonable costs from the person who could be fined [bc.spa.s133] — after the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135].

**The limit.** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the *Human Rights Code* [bc.spa.s121]. Hoarding frequently engages it.

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 3 supplies the nuisance and hazard grounds and standard bylaw 7 the entry rights [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3, bc.spa.sched.bylaw7]. See [building.standard-bylaw.7].

## Common mistakes

- Entering without the notice the bylaw requires, outside a genuine emergency [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7].
- Removing possessions from inside a strata lot as self-help, rather than under the remedy power and after the process [bc.spa.s133, bc.spa.s135].
- Doing work under a work order without the week's notice or while an appeal is on foot [bc.spa.s85].
- Running enforcement while an accommodation request sits unanswered [bc.spa.s121].
- Escalating to fines where the hazard needs the remedy power instead.
