# Parking in a strata building

## Summary

Every parking question starts with what the stall legally is: part of a strata lot, limited common property, common property, or a common asset. The strata plan decides that, not the bylaw. Where a stall is common property, the corporation can grant exclusive use for only one year at a time, renewable [bc.spa.s76] — a permanent allocation needs a designation of limited common property instead [bc.spa.s73, bc.spa.s74].

## What the law says

**Assignment.** Subject to the rule on significant changes, the corporation may give an owner or tenant permission to exclusively use, or a special privilege in relation to, common assets or common property that is not limited common property. That permission may be conditional, lasts no more than one year, may be renewed on changed terms, and may be cancelled on reasonable notice [bc.spa.s76]. The one prescribed exception is a stall where EV charging infrastructure was installed following an owner's request, where the period is 5 years [bc.spr.s5.101].

**Permanent allocation.** That is a designation of limited common property: by the owner developer on deposit or by plan amendment, by amendment to the strata plan, or by a 3/4 vote resolution filed in the land title office with a sketch plan defining the areas and specifying which strata lots have the exclusive use [bc.spa.s73, bc.spa.s74].

**Charging for parking.** A user fee. The corporation must not impose user fees for the use of common property or common assets except as the Regulation allows: the amount must be reasonable and set out in a bylaw, or in a rule that has been ratified. It may be a fixed amount or set on a reasonable basis such as consumption, recovery of operating or maintenance costs, the number of users or the duration of use [bc.spa.s110, bc.spr.s6.9].

**Changing the parking area.** A significant change in the use or appearance of common property needs a 3/4 vote, or a majority vote where it relates to EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, unless immediate change is needed for safety or to prevent significant loss or damage [bc.spa.s71].

**Rules and bylaws.** Rules reach common property stalls but not stalls inside a strata lot [bc.spa.s125]; everything else rests on the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119].

## What the standard bylaws say

No standard bylaw deals with parking. A building with only the standard bylaws has the use bylaw and the alteration bylaws, and nothing that assigns stalls. The bylaw shapes are in [building.bylaw-pattern.parking].

## Common mistakes

- Treating a long-standing stall assignment as permanent when it is a year-to-year permission [bc.spa.s76].
- Charging for a common property stall without a bylaw or a ratified rule setting a reasonable fee [bc.spr.s6.9].
- Reassigning a stall as a sanction. That is not one of the Act's enforcement options.
- Purporting to reassign a stall that is limited common property or part of a strata lot [bc.spa.s73].
- Converting visitor parking to assigned parking without asking whether it is a significant change [bc.spa.s71].
