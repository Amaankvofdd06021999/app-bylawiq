# The contingency reserve fund

## Summary

Two funds, with different purposes: the operating fund for expenses that occur once a year or more often, plus the cost of obtaining a depreciation report or an electrical planning report, and the contingency reserve fund for expenses that occur less often than once a year or not usually at all [bc.spa.s92]. The minimum annual contribution to the reserve fund is at least 10% of the operating fund budget [bc.spr.s6.1].

## What the law says

**The two funds.** The corporation must establish both, and owners contribute to both through strata fees [bc.spa.s92].

**Contributions.** The corporation determines the annual contribution to the reserve fund subject to the regulations [bc.spa.s93]. That amount must be determined after considering the most recent depreciation report, if any, and must be at least 10% of the total budgeted contribution to the operating fund for the current fiscal year — except for the fiscal year following the first annual general meeting [bc.spr.s6.1, bc.spa.s94].

**Holding it.** Reserve fund money must be accounted for separately and invested in investments permitted by the regulations or in insured accounts with savings institutions in British Columbia [bc.spa.s95].

**Spending it.** Only where the expenditure is consistent with the fund's purpose and approved as the Act requires: a **majority vote** where it is necessary to obtain a depreciation report or an electrical planning report, relates to repair, maintenance or replacement recommended in the most current depreciation report of common property, common assets or portions of a strata lot the corporation has taken responsibility for, relates to installing EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, or is necessary for another report about that infrastructure; and a **3/4 vote** otherwise [bc.spa.s96]. Operating fund spending needs a 3/4 vote or authorisation in the budget or under the unapproved-expenditure provisions [bc.spa.s97], and an unapproved expenditure from the operating fund is limited to the amount in the bylaws or, if silent, the lesser of $2,000 and 5% of the operating fund contribution for the year [bc.spa.s98].

**Deductibles.** Approval is not required for a reserve fund expenditure to cover an insurance deductible the corporation must pay to repair or replace damaged property, unless it has decided not to repair [bc.spa.s158].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 21 bars anyone from spending the corporation's money without a delegated power, except that a council member may spend to repair or replace common property or common assets where the work is immediately required to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage. That is a bylaw limit sitting on top of the statutory approval rules, not a substitute for them.

## Common mistakes

- Budgeting a reserve contribution below the regulated minimum [bc.spr.s6.1].
- Setting the contribution without considering the most recent depreciation report [bc.spr.s6.1].
- Using a 3/4 vote where a majority vote suffices, or a majority where 3/4 is needed: which one depends on whether the work is recommended in the most current depreciation report [bc.spa.s96].
- Mixing reserve money with other funds [bc.spa.s95].
- Treating the unapproved expenditure limit as a general spending allowance [bc.spa.s98].
