# Council hearings

## Summary

An owner or tenant may request a hearing at a council meeting by written application stating the reason, and council must hold one within four weeks; if the purpose was to seek a decision, council must give a written decision within a week of the hearing [bc.spa.s34.1]. A hearing means being heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2]. The same right appears inside the bylaw enforcement process, where a hearing must be offered if requested before a penalty [bc.spa.s135].

## What the law says

**The right.** By application in writing stating the reason for the request, an owner or tenant may request a hearing at a council meeting. Council must hold a council meeting to hear the applicant within four weeks after the request. If the purpose of the hearing is to seek a decision of council, council must give the applicant a written decision within one week after the hearing [bc.spa.s34.1].

**What a hearing is.** An opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2]. That definition is given for the enforcement process, where the corporation must give a reasonable opportunity to answer a complaint, including a hearing if requested by the owner or tenant [bc.spa.s135].

**Two different hearings, one meaning.** A hearing requested under [bc.spa.s34.1] can be about anything; a hearing requested under [bc.spa.s135] is part of answering a complaint before a penalty. The four-week deadline and the one-week written decision attach to the first [bc.spa.s34.1]; the requirement to give written notice of the decision as soon as feasible attaches to the second [bc.spa.s135].

**Getting council together.** Under the standard bylaws any council member may call a council meeting on one week's notice, shorter with consent or in an emergency, and council must tell owners about a called meeting as soon as feasible [bc.spa.sched.bylaw14]. A building that has lengthened that notice period has made the four-week deadline harder to meet.

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 17 lets owners attend council meetings as observers, but not the parts dealing with a bylaw contravention hearing, or with any other matter where council considers that observers would unreasonably interfere with someone's privacy [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17]. It also allows council to meet by electronic means where everyone can communicate with each other, and deems members present in person when it does — which matters when a hearing has to be held inside four weeks [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17]. Council must still inform owners of the minutes within two weeks [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19]. What a building can change is in [building.standard-bylaw.17].

## Common mistakes

- Treating a request as invalid because it did not use a form. The Act asks for a written application stating the reason [bc.spa.s34.1].
- Missing the four-week deadline because council does not meet often enough [bc.spa.s34.1, bc.spa.sched.bylaw14].
- Holding the hearing but never sending the written decision, where the purpose was to seek one [bc.spa.s34.1].
- Offering a written exchange instead of being heard in person [bc.spr.s7.2].
- Holding the hearing in open session with other owners present [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17].
- Recording the substance of a closed hearing in minutes that go to all owners [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17, bc.spa.sched.bylaw19].
- Imposing the penalty at the same meeting without letting the person be heard first [bc.spa.s135].
