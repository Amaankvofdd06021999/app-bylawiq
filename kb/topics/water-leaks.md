# Water leaks between strata lots and from common property

## Summary

Three questions decide a leak, and they are separate: who had to repair the thing that failed [bc.spa.s72], whose insurance responds and who bears the deductible [bc.spa.s149, bc.spa.s158], and whether anyone is answerable for causing the loss [bc.spa.s158]. Fault is the last of the three, not the first. The strata plan decides the first, because it fixes what is a strata lot, common property or limited common property.

## What the law says

**Who repairs.** The strata corporation must repair and maintain common property and common assets. By bylaw it may make an owner responsible for limited common property the owner may use, and for other common property only where the Regulation identifies it and subject to the prescribed restrictions; and it may take on responsibility for specified portions of a strata lot [bc.spa.s72]. Where it has taken on portions of some lots but not others, the cost is shared only among the lots it relates to, by a different formula [bc.spr.s6.5].

**Insurance.** The corporation must obtain and maintain property insurance on common property, common assets, the buildings shown on the strata plan, and fixtures built or installed on a strata lot by the owner developer as part of the original construction, generally on a full replacement value basis [bc.spa.s149]. It may insure other perils and other fixtures [bc.spa.s152]. Whatever the policy says, the named insureds include the corporation, the owners and tenants from time to time, and the persons who normally occupy the strata lots [bc.spa.s155]. It must review the adequacy of its insurance annually, report at each annual general meeting and tell owners and tenants as soon as feasible of a material change, including an increase in a deductible [bc.spa.s154].

**The deductible.** Paying an insurance deductible on a claim on the corporation's insurance is a common expense, contributed to through strata fees [bc.spa.s158]. That does not stop the corporation suing an owner to recover the deductible where the owner is responsible for the loss or damage that gave rise to the claim [bc.spa.s158]. And approval is not needed for a special levy or a contingency reserve fund expenditure to cover a deductible the corporation must pay to repair or replace damaged property, unless it has decided not to repair [bc.spa.s158].

**Access and remedy.** Where work is needed, the standard bylaws require entry to be allowed in an emergency without notice, and otherwise on 48 hours' written notice for work the corporation is responsible for [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7]. The corporation may do what is reasonably necessary to remedy a contravention of its bylaws, including doing work on a strata lot, and may require the reasonable costs from the person who could be fined [bc.spa.s133].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaws 2 and 8 divide the repair duty. The owner repairs and maintains the strata lot and any limited common property they have the use of, except what the bylaws make the corporation's [bc.spa.sched.bylaw2]. The corporation repairs and maintains common assets; common property that is not limited common property; limited common property for work that in the ordinary course happens less often than once a year plus a listed set of building elements; and, in a plan that is not bare land, that same listed set within a strata lot — structure, exterior, things attached to the exterior, exterior doors, windows and skylights, and fences, railings and similar structures enclosing patios, balconies and yards [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8]. Standard bylaw 7 governs entry [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7]. See [building.standard-bylaw.8] and [building.standard-bylaw.7].

## Common mistakes

- Starting with fault. The first question is who had the repair obligation [bc.spa.s72].
- Charging the deductible to an owner as though the Act made them liable for it. It is a common expense unless the owner is responsible for the loss, and recovering it is a claim, not an automatic charge [bc.spa.s158].
- Overlooking that owners, tenants and normal occupants are named insureds [bc.spa.s155].
- Assuming a balcony or patio is the owner's simply because only they use it. Limited common property is split between owner and corporation by the standard bylaws [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8].
- Entering a strata lot without the notice the bylaws require, outside an emergency [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7].
- Treating a deductible expenditure as needing an owners' vote when the Act says it does not [bc.spa.s158].
