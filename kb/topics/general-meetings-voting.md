# General meetings, notice, quorum and voting

## Summary

Notice is two weeks, or four for a winding-up resolution [bc.spa.s45]. Quorum is eligible voters holding 1/3 of the votes, present in person or by proxy, subject to the bylaws [bc.spa.s48]. Matters are decided by majority vote unless the Act or regulations require or permit a different threshold [bc.spa.s50]. A 3/4 vote carried by fewer than half the votes can be forced back to a second meeting [bc.spa.s51].

## What the law says

**Notice.** At least two weeks' written notice of an annual or special general meeting to every owner, to every mortgagee who has filed a Mortgagee's Request for Notification, and to every tenant assigned a landlord's right to vote where the corporation has notice of the assignment; four weeks where a winding-up resolution will be considered [bc.spa.s45].

**Quorum.** Subject to the bylaws, eligible voters holding 1/3 of the votes, present in person or by proxy, or 2/3 where there are fewer than 4 strata lots or fewer than 4 owners; no business may be conducted without it [bc.spa.s48].

**Voting.** Majority vote unless a different threshold is required or permitted. The wording of a 3/4 vote resolution may be amended during the meeting if the amendment does not substantially change it and is approved by a 3/4 vote first [bc.spa.s50]. Each strata lot has one vote unless a Schedule of Voting Rights provides otherwise, and a bylaw may suspend the vote of a lot the corporation could lien, except on 80% and unanimous votes [bc.spa.s53]. A person entitled to vote may vote in person or by proxy [bc.spa.s56].

**Reconsideration.** Where a resolution requiring a 3/4 vote passes with less than 50% of the corporation's votes, the corporation must not act on it for one week unless immediate action is needed for safety or to prevent significant loss or damage, and within that week holders of at least 25% of the votes may demand a special general meeting to reconsider it [bc.spa.s51]. Where a unanimous vote falls short by one lot, or by lots holding under 5% of the votes, a corporation of 10 or more lots may apply to court by 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s52].

**What happens at an annual general meeting.** The budget is approved by majority vote, distributed with the notice and accompanied by a financial statement [bc.spa.s103]; insurance coverage is reported [bc.spa.s154]; new rules are ratified or cease to have effect [bc.spa.s125]; bylaw amendments need a 3/4 vote and filing [bc.spa.s128]. Voters may also call a special general meeting [bc.spa.s43].

## What the standard bylaws say

Standard bylaw 25 puts the president in the chair, then the vice president, then a chair elected from those present [bc.spa.sched.bylaw25]. Standard bylaw 27 sets voting cards, a show of cards unless a precise count is asked for, announcement and minuting of results, a presidential tie-break except in a two-lot plan, and a secret ballot on any eligible voter's request [bc.spa.sched.bylaw27]. Standard bylaw 28 sets the order of business [bc.spa.sched.bylaw28].

## Common mistakes

- Short notice, or notice that omits mortgagees who filed a request or tenants with an assignment [bc.spa.s45].
- Conducting business without quorum [bc.spa.s48].
- Substantially rewriting a 3/4 vote resolution from the floor [bc.spa.s50].
- Acting immediately on a 3/4 vote carried by under half the votes [bc.spa.s51].
- Refusing a request for a secret ballot [bc.spa.sched.bylaw27].
- Treating an amendment as effective on the vote rather than on filing [bc.spa.s128].
