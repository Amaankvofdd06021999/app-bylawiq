# Standard bylaw 10: how long a council member serves

## What it covers

Ends a council member's term at the close of the annual general meeting at which the new council is elected, and makes a departing member eligible for re-election. Its subsections (3) to (5) were repealed in 1999 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw10].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 10 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Some buildings introduce two year or staggered terms so that the whole council does not turn over at once, and a few add term limits. The election itself is governed by the Act [bc.spa.s25], so an amendment here changes the length of a term, not the way the vote is run. A staggered scheme has to say what happens in the first year it starts, or the building ends up with an uncertain council.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether terms are one year or longer, whether they are staggered, whether there is a term limit, and what the bylaw says about a member elected part way through a term.
