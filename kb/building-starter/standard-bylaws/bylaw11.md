# Standard bylaw 11: removing a council member

## What it covers

Lets the owners remove one or more council members by majority vote at an annual or special general meeting, unless all the owners are on council, and requires an election at the same meeting to fill the seat for the rest of the term [bc.spa.sched.bylaw11].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 11 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

This bylaw is rarely amended, and raising the threshold above a majority vote is the change to look for. Note what it does not cover: a member who resigns or cannot act is dealt with under standard bylaw 12, not this one [bc.spa.sched.bylaw12], and this bylaw gives council no power to remove one of its own members — only the owners at a general meeting can.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The vote threshold; whether the bylaw still requires the replacement election at the same meeting; and whether anything purports to let council remove a member, which the standard scheme does not allow.
