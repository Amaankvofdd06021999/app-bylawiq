# Standard bylaw 14: calling a council meeting

## What it covers

Lets any council member call a council meeting on at least one week's notice to the other members, with the reason stated; the notice need not be in writing. A shorter notice works if all members consent in advance, or if the meeting deals with an emergency and all members either consent in advance or cannot be reached after reasonable attempts. Council must tell owners about a called meeting as soon as feasible [bc.spa.sched.bylaw14].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 14 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments usually require written notice and set out how it is given. Two deadlines elsewhere in the scheme depend on council being able to meet promptly: a hearing requested by an owner or tenant must be held within four weeks of the request, and a decision given within a week of the hearing where the hearing was to seek one [bc.spa.s34.1]. A bylaw that lengthened the notice period without thinking about that makes the four weeks harder to meet.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The notice period and whether notice must be written; how the emergency route is worded; and whether owners are in fact told about council meetings, which the standard bylaw requires.
