# Standard bylaw 7: entering a strata lot

## What it covers

Requires an owner, tenant, occupant or visitor to let a person authorised by the strata corporation into the strata lot in two situations: in an emergency, with no notice, to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage; and at a reasonable time on 48 hours' written notice, to inspect, repair or maintain common property, common assets and the parts of a lot the corporation must repair, maintain or insure [bc.spa.s149]. The notice must give the date, the approximate time and the reason [bc.spa.sched.bylaw7].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 7 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings sometimes lengthen the notice, add a right of entry for inspections on a schedule, or set out what happens if entry is refused. The limits worth knowing: entry without notice is confined to the emergency ground in the bylaw, and the 48 hour ground is tied to work the corporation is responsible for, so a bylaw that gave a general right to enter for any reason would go beyond the standard scheme. Any bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. Where an occupant will not allow entry, the answer is the Act's enforcement route and, if needed, an order; it is not self-help.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The notice period and what the notice must say; whether a routine inspection right has been added and how often; and whether the bylaw tries to authorise entry for matters the corporation is not responsible for repairing or insuring.
