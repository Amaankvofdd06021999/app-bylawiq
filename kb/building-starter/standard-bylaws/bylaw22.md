# Standard bylaw 22: when a council member is personally protected

## What it covers

Protects a council member who acts honestly and in good faith from personal liability for anything done or not done in exercising, or intending to exercise, a power or duty of council, and makes clear that the protection does not affect the member's liability as an owner for a judgment against the corporation [bc.spa.sched.bylaw22].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 22 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments are rare and buildings should be careful: this protection is conditional on acting honestly and in good faith, which is the first half of the standard of care the Act imposes [bc.spa.s31]. It does not cover a failure to disclose a conflict of interest [bc.spa.s32], and it is not insurance. A bylaw cannot make the corporation indemnify a member for conduct the Act makes them answerable for.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the registered bylaw goes further than the standard protection, for example by promising an indemnity or legal costs; and whether the building's insurance covers council members, since the bylaw itself pays nothing.
