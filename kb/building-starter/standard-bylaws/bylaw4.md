# Standard bylaw 4: telling the strata corporation who lives here

## What it covers

Requires a new owner to give the strata corporation their name, strata lot number and a mailing address outside the strata plan within two weeks of becoming an owner, and a tenant to give their name when the corporation asks [bc.spa.sched.bylaw4].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 4 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings often widen this to ask for emergency contacts, vehicle and parking details, occupant counts or tenancy dates. Two things bound it. The corporation already has to prepare and keep a list of owners and their addresses, strata lot numbers, parking and storage locker numbers, unit entitlements and the names of tenants [bc.spa.s35], and owners can ask to see those records [bc.spa.s36], so information collected under this bylaw is information other owners may be entitled to see. Any bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. Whether collecting more than the Act requires is permitted is a privacy question the kb cannot answer yet: the *Personal Information Protection Act* is not in it (open question 29). Separately, a landlord's duty to give a tenant the bylaws and rules and a signed Notice of Tenant's Responsibilities, and to pass that notice to the corporation within two weeks, comes from the Act and not from this bylaw [bc.spa.s146].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

What the registered bylaw asks for beyond the standard list, and whether the building has a privacy policy covering it. If the bylaw demands information the corporation then has to disclose on a records request, say so before collecting it.
