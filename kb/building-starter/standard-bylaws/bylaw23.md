# Standard bylaw 23: the maximum fine, and why buildings change it

## What it covers

Sets the maximum the corporation may fine an owner or tenant at $50 for each contravention of a bylaw and $10 for each contravention of a rule [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 23 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

This is the standard bylaw most worth checking, because the Act requires the corporation to set its maximum fine in its bylaws and caps that maximum at the figures in the Regulation [bc.spa.s132], and those figures are much higher than the standard bylaw's: $200 for each contravention of a bylaw, $50 for each contravention of a rule, and $1,000 for each contravention of a bylaw prohibiting or limiting the use of all or part of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation [bc.spr.s7.1]. A building that has never amended this bylaw is limited to $50 and $10 no matter what the Regulation allows. The bylaws may also set different maximums for different bylaws and rules [bc.spa.s132]. How often a fine may be repeated is standard bylaw 24 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw24].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The maximum in the registered bylaws, and whether it is at or under the regulated cap (a bylaw figure above the cap is ineffective to the extent of the excess [bc.spa.s132]); whether different maximums are set for different bylaws; and whether a short-term accommodation bylaw exists to which the higher figure could attach.
