# Standard bylaws

The verbatim text of the Schedule of Standard Bylaws lives in the law layer, one `schedule` item per standard bylaw, at `law/bc/acts/strata-property-act/schedule-of-standard-bylaws/` (ids `bc.spa.sched.bylaw1` to `bc.spa.sched.bylaw30`; bylaw 15 is repealed). It is not copied here, so retrieval never sees the same passage twice. See that folder's README for the reasoning.

Add a `standard-bylaw` item here only when it says something of its own about how a standard bylaw works for a building (for example: it applies unless the building's registered bylaws replace it, and what to check in the registered bylaws). Such an item cites the matching `bc.spa.sched.bylaw*` item, does not restate the bylaw text, and needs counsel review (research plan R7).

## What is here

One note per standard bylaw, `bylaw<n>.md`, id `building.standard-bylaw.<n>`, for each of the 29 standard bylaws (there is no bylaw 15; it was repealed). Each has the same four headings, which are also the chunk boundaries:

- **What it covers** — the subject matter of the bylaw, in our own words, with the text left in the law item.
- **When it applies to your building** — the same point in every note: the standard bylaw applies unless a different bylaw on that subject is filed in the land title office, so the registered bylaws are what matter.
- **What a building can change, and the limits** — the amendments a building can make and the provisions of the Act and Regulation that bound them.
- **What to check in the registered bylaws** — what to look for when reading a building's own bylaws against this one.

Where a note says a change is common, that is reasoning from the Act and the Regulation, not observed practice. Observed patterns belong in `../bylaw-patterns/` (task B3), with the decisions that tested them (Phase C). Each note's `notes` field says so, and every note is `draft` and needs counsel review.
