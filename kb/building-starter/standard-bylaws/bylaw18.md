# Standard bylaw 18: voting at council meetings

## What it covers

Requires council decisions to be made by a majority of the members present in person, lets the president break a tie with a second, deciding vote unless there are only two strata lots in the plan, and requires the result of every vote to be recorded in the minutes [bc.spa.sched.bylaw18].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 18 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments are uncommon. Two duties sit alongside this bylaw and cannot be voted away: each council member must act honestly and in good faith in the best interests of the corporation and with the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person [bc.spa.s31], and a member must disclose a conflict of interest on the terms the Act sets [bc.spa.s32].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The majority and tie-break wording; whether votes are actually recorded in the minutes, which this bylaw requires and which is the first thing a challenge looks for; and whether conflicts are recorded when they arise.
