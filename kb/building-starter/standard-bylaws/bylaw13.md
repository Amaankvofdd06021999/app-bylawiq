# Standard bylaw 13: council officers

## What it covers

Requires council, at its first meeting after each annual general meeting, to elect a president, vice president, secretary and treasurer from among its members; allows one person to hold more than one office except president and vice president together; gives the vice president the president's powers while the president cannot act or for the rest of the term if the president leaves; and lets council replace another officer who cannot act for two months or more [bc.spa.sched.bylaw13].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 13 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings sometimes add officers or merge the secretary and treasurer roles formally. Who holds these offices matters beyond council: the president chairs general meetings and, with the vice president, holds the tie-breaking vote [bc.spa.sched.bylaw25]. A building with no properly elected president can find its general meetings and tie votes open to challenge.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Which offices the registered bylaws require; whether the first-meeting timing is kept; and whether the minutes of the council meeting after the last annual general meeting actually record the election of officers.
