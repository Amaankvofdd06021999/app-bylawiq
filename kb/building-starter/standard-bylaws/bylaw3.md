# Standard bylaw 3: use of property, noise, nuisance and pets

## What it covers

The busiest standard bylaw. It sets out the limits on how a strata lot, the common property and the common assets may be used: no nuisance or hazard to another person, no unreasonable noise, no unreasonable interference with other people's use and enjoyment, nothing illegal, and nothing contrary to the purpose the strata plan shows for the lot or the common property. It also forbids damage beyond reasonable wear and tear to common property and to the parts of a strata lot the corporation must repair or insure [bc.spa.s149], requires animals to be leashed or secured on common property, and sets a pet allowance [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 3 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Almost every building rewrites part of this bylaw, usually the pet allowance or the noise wording. Two limits on a pet bylaw are absolute. A bylaw that prohibits a pet does not apply to a pet that was already living with an owner, tenant or occupant without contravening any pet bylaw immediately before the new bylaw passed, for as long as that pet keeps living there [bc.spa.s123]. And a bylaw prohibiting or restricting an animal never applies to a guide dog or service dog, or to the dog of a retired guide or service dog team where the handler is an owner, tenant or occupant [bc.spa.s123]; those terms come from the *Guide Dog and Service Dog Act*, which the kb does not hold yet (see open question 32). Any bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. The *Human Rights Code* limit is what makes an assistance animal or a disability-related noise complaint a different question from an ordinary pet or noise complaint.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Which pets are allowed and how many; whether the bylaw carries its own grandfathering wording, and that the wording is not narrower than the Act (the Act's protection applies whatever the bylaw says); whether quiet hours have been added, and whether the noise standard is still "unreasonable" or has become an absolute one; and whether the building has rules about the common property that sit alongside this bylaw.
