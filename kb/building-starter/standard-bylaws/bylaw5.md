# Standard bylaw 5: approval before altering a strata lot

## What it covers

Requires an owner to get the strata corporation's written approval before altering a strata lot in the listed ways: the structure or exterior of a building; chimneys, stairs, balconies and other things attached to the exterior; exterior doors, windows and skylights, and those fronting on common property; fences, railings and similar structures enclosing a patio, balcony or yard; common property inside the lot's boundaries; and the parts of the lot the corporation must insure [bc.spa.s149]. The corporation must not unreasonably withhold approval, but may require the owner to take responsibility in writing for the expenses of the alteration. It does not apply to a lot in a bare land strata plan [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 5 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings commonly add process to this bylaw: a written application, drawings, contractor insurance, a deposit, hours of work, and a signed alteration or indemnity agreement. Adding process is straightforward; narrowing the owner's position is not, because the standard "must not unreasonably withhold" wording is in the bylaw itself and a building that keeps the standard bylaw keeps that duty.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the list of alterations needing approval is longer than the standard one; whether an alteration agreement is required and what it makes the owner liable for; whether a deadline for the corporation to answer has been added; and, for a bare land plan, whether the building has filed a bylaw of its own, since the standard bylaw does not apply there.
