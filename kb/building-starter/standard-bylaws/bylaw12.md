# Standard bylaw 12: replacing a council member between meetings

## What it covers

Lets the remaining council members appoint a replacement for the rest of the term when a member resigns or is unwilling or unable to act for two months or more, from anyone eligible to sit on council, and allows the appointment even where the absence has left council without a quorum. If every member goes, people holding at least 25% of the votes may call a special general meeting to elect a new council [bc.spa.sched.bylaw12].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 12 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments are uncommon. The practical point is the two month trigger: before it is reached, council has no power to appoint under this bylaw. Eligibility still comes from the Act [bc.spa.s28], so an appointee must be someone who could have been elected.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the two month period has been shortened; whether the quorum override survives; and whether the 25% threshold for a rescue meeting has changed.
