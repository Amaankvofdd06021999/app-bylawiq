# Standard bylaw 27: how votes are taken at a general meeting

## What it covers

Sets the mechanics of voting at annual and special general meetings: voting cards to eligible voters; a show of voting cards unless a voter asks for a precise count; the chair choosing the method of a precise count; announcing and minuting the outcome, with the numbers for and against where a precise count was asked for; a tie broken by the president, or the vice president if the president is absent or will not vote, except in a two-lot plan; and a secret ballot for a council election or any other vote if an eligible voter asks for one [bc.spa.sched.bylaw27].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 27 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments usually deal with electronic and advance voting. What cannot be amended away sits in the Act: who may vote and how votes are counted [bc.spa.s50], and the rules for proxies [bc.spa.s56]. The secret ballot on request is the provision most often overlooked, and refusing a properly made request puts the whole vote at risk.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether electronic or advance voting has been added and how it interacts with the secret ballot right; whether tie-break wording survives; and whether the minutes record outcomes the way this bylaw requires.
