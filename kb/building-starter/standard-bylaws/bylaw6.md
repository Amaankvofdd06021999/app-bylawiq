# Standard bylaw 6: approval before altering common property

## What it covers

Requires an owner to get written approval before altering common property, including limited common property, or common assets, and lets the corporation require the owner to take responsibility in writing for the expenses [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6]. Unlike standard bylaw 5 there is no express duty not to withhold approval unreasonably.

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 6 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

The usual amendments add an application process and an indemnity agreement, and set out standing approvals for small things such as a balcony screen or a satellite dish. The limit sits outside this bylaw: a significant change in the use or appearance of common property or of land that is a common asset needs a 3/4 vote at a general meeting, or a majority vote where the change relates to EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, unless immediate change is needed for safety or to prevent significant loss or damage [bc.spa.s71]. Council cannot approve under this bylaw something that is really a significant change needing the owners' vote. One kind of owner request has its own route in the Act: an owner may ask the corporation to approve alterations to common property, or to land that is a common asset, that are necessary to install EV charging infrastructure for use at a parking stall, and the corporation may approve that request where any significant change has been approved by the resolution the Act requires and the prescribed criteria are met [bc.spa.s90.1, bc.spa.s90.2].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the registered bylaw sets a process and an indemnity; whether it purports to let council approve changes to the appearance of the building, and if so whether the change would be significant enough to need a vote [bc.spa.s71]; and how limited common property such as balconies and patios is treated.
