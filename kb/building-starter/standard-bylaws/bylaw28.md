# Standard bylaw 28: the order of business at a general meeting

## What it covers

Sets the order of business at annual and special general meetings, from certifying proxies and issuing voting cards, through quorum, electing a chair if needed, proof of notice, the agenda, the previous minutes and unfinished business, to council's reports, ratifying new rules [bc.spa.s125], reporting on insurance [bc.spa.s154], approving the budget [bc.spa.s103], new business including matters notice was given for [bc.spa.s45], electing council, and termination [bc.spa.sched.bylaw28].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 28 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments are usually cosmetic reordering. Several items on the list are statutory duties that happen at the annual general meeting whether or not the bylaw lists them, so dropping one from the order of business does not remove the duty: rules cease to have effect at the first annual general meeting after they are made unless ratified [bc.spa.s125], and the budget has to be approved [bc.spa.s103]. The new business item is tied to the Act's notice requirements [bc.spa.s45]: a resolution needing notice cannot be rescued by raising it from the floor.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the registered order still includes rule ratification, the insurance report and budget approval; and whether the notice package for the last meeting matched what was actually voted on.
