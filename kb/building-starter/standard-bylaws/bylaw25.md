# Standard bylaw 25: who chairs a general meeting

## What it covers

Puts the president of council in the chair at annual and special general meetings, the vice president if the president is unwilling or unable to act, and otherwise a chair elected by the eligible voters present in person or by proxy from among those at the meeting [bc.spa.sched.bylaw25].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 25 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Some buildings allow an outside chair, such as the strata manager or a lawyer, for a contentious meeting; that requires an amendment, because the standard bylaw confines the choice to people present at the meeting. The chair's identity matters because the president and vice president hold the tie-breaking vote at a general meeting [bc.spa.sched.bylaw27], and both offices come from standard bylaw 13 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw13].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether an outside chair is permitted; and, if the meeting elected its own chair, whether the minutes record that election as the first item.
