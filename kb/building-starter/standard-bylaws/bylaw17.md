# Standard bylaw 17: council meetings, electronic attendance and observers

## What it covers

Lets council meet by electronic means at its option so long as everyone taking part can communicate with each other, and deems members present in person when it does [bc.spa.sched.bylaw16]. Owners may attend council meetings as observers, except for the parts dealing with a bylaw contravention hearing under the Act [bc.spa.s135] or with any other matter where council thinks observers would unreasonably interfere with someone's privacy [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 17 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings often add how observers are admitted, whether they may speak and how a closed portion is recorded. The parts that carry consequences are the closed-session rules, because a bylaw contravention hearing is where the corporation's procedural duties bite: a hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2], and the corporation must give the person the particulars of the complaint in writing and a reasonable opportunity to answer it, including a hearing if requested, before it fines or charges them [bc.spa.s135].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the bylaw still allows electronic meetings and still deems attendance in person; how observers are handled; and whether minutes distinguish the closed portion so a later challenge can be answered without disclosing the hearing.
