# Standard bylaw 9: how many people sit on council

## What it covers

Sets council at no fewer than three and no more than seven members, except that where the strata plan has fewer than four strata lots, or the corporation has fewer than four owners, every owner is on council [bc.spa.sched.bylaw9].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 9 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Larger buildings sometimes raise the ceiling, and two-lot and three-lot buildings usually leave this alone because the all-owners rule already fits them. Council size drives quorum, so an amendment here changes standard bylaw 16 in effect even if that bylaw is untouched [bc.spa.sched.bylaw16]. Who may sit on council is a separate question and comes from the Act: owners, individuals representing corporate owners, and tenants who have been assigned the landlord's right to stand. A bylaw passed after the first annual general meeting may let other classes of person sit, and a bylaw may bar a person in respect of a strata lot the corporation could register a lien against [bc.spa.s28].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The minimum and maximum in the registered bylaws, and whether the building has a bylaw widening or restricting eligibility under [bc.spa.s28]. If the council has fewer members than the bylaw's minimum, decisions may be open to challenge, so check the last election result too.
