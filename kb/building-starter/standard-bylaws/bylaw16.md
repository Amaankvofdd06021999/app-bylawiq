# Standard bylaw 16: quorum of council

## What it covers

Sets council quorum by council size: one where council has one member, two where it has two, three or four, three where it has five or six, and four where it has seven. Members must be present in person to count towards quorum [bc.spa.sched.bylaw16]. There is no standard bylaw 15; it was repealed.

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 16 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Quorum follows council size, so amending standard bylaw 9 changes the effect of this bylaw [bc.spa.sched.bylaw9]. "Present in person" is less restrictive than it looks: where council chooses to meet by electronic means and everyone can communicate with each other, members are deemed present in person [bc.spa.sched.bylaw17]. A building that has amended bylaw 17 should check that the deeming provision survived.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

The quorum numbers against the council size the bylaws allow; and whether the electronic meeting deeming provision is still in place if council meets remotely.
