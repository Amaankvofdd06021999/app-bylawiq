# Standard bylaw 26: tenants and others who cannot vote

## What it covers

Lets tenants and occupants attend annual and special general meetings whether or not they may vote, allows people who cannot vote to join the discussion only if the chair permits, and requires them to leave if a majority vote at the meeting asks them to [bc.spa.sched.bylaw26].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 26 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Amendments are uncommon. The related point is that a tenant may be able to vote: a landlord may assign powers and duties to a tenant, effective only once the corporation has written notice naming the tenant, the powers assigned and the period [bc.spa.s147]. Check the assignment notices before treating a tenant as a non-voter.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the registered bylaw restricts attendance more than the standard one; and whether the building holds any landlord-to-tenant assignment notices, which change who may vote and who may stand for council.
