# Standard bylaw 29: the voluntary dispute resolution committee

## What it covers

Lets a dispute among owners, tenants, the corporation or any combination of them go to a dispute resolution committee where every party consents and the dispute involves the Act, the regulations, the bylaws or the rules; sets out how the committee is made up, either one nominee for each disputing party plus a chair chosen by those nominees, or any people or method all parties consent to; and requires the committee to try to help the parties end the dispute voluntarily [bc.spa.sched.bylaw29].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 29 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

Buildings sometimes drop this bylaw or replace it with a mediation clause. Whatever it says, it cannot close off anyone's other routes: using a voluntary dispute resolution process under the bylaws does not affect a person's powers, duties or rights, including the right to sue, to begin arbitration or mediation, or to ask the Civil Resolution Tribunal to resolve a claim [bc.spa.s124]. A bylaw that made this committee a precondition to a tribunal claim would not have that effect.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the bylaw survives and on what terms; and whether it is presented to owners as optional, because describing it as a required first step misstates the position [bc.spa.s124].
