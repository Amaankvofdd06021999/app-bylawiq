# Standard bylaw 1: when strata fees are due

## What it covers

Sets the payment schedule for strata fees: the day of the month by which an owner must pay the fees for that month [bc.spa.sched.bylaw1]. It says nothing about how the fees are calculated, what happens if they are late, or how arrears are collected.

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 1 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

A building can set a different schedule, and this is one of the few bylaws where amending has a direct financial consequence. A bylaw that establishes a payment schedule may also charge interest on late fees, but only up to 10% per annum compounded annually [bc.spa.s107, bc.spr.s6.8]. Without such a bylaw there is no interest to charge. That interest is not a fine, and it counts as strata fees, which means it can go onto a Certificate of Lien [bc.spa.s107, bc.spa.s116]. A rate above the cap is unenforceable to the extent of the excess. Any bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the due date differs from the first of the month; whether there is an interest bylaw at all, and at what rate; and whether that rate is stated per annum or per month (a monthly rate can breach the annual cap). Collection itself runs off the Act, not this bylaw.
