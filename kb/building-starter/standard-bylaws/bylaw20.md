# Standard bylaw 20: what council may delegate, and what it may not

## What it covers

Lets council delegate its powers and duties to council members or to others, and revoke the delegation; allows spending powers to be delegated only by a resolution that either authorises a specific amount for a specific purpose or gives general authority with a maximum amount and the purposes or conditions; and forbids delegating three decisions that turn on the facts of a case: whether a person contravened a bylaw or rule, whether they should be fined and how much, and whether they should be denied access to a recreational facility [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 20 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

The delegation to a strata manager is the reason this bylaw matters in practice, and the three forbidden delegations are the ones a manager can breach without noticing. Those three decisions map onto the corporation's enforcement options — a fine, remedying a contravention, denying a recreational facility [bc.spa.s130, bc.spa.s133, bc.spa.s134] — and they must be council's own decisions, taken after the corporation has given the person written particulars of the complaint and a reasonable opportunity to answer [bc.spa.s135]. A manager may prepare and send correspondence; the decision to fine has to be council's, recorded as such. Spending delegation also runs with standard bylaw 21 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw21].

## What to check in the registered bylaws

What has been delegated and by what resolution; whether any spending delegation states a maximum and a purpose; and whether the minutes show council, not the manager, deciding each fine. A delegation that offends the three limits is the most common ground for setting a fine aside.
