# Standard bylaw 8: what the strata corporation must repair and maintain

## What it covers

Sets the corporation's repair and maintenance duty in four tiers: common assets; common property that is not limited common property; limited common property, but only for work that in the ordinary course happens less often than once a year, plus a listed set of building elements however often they need work; and, in a plan that is not a bare land plan, a strata lot, but only for that same listed set of building elements — structure, exterior, things attached to the exterior, exterior doors, windows and skylights and those fronting on common property, and fences, railings and similar structures enclosing patios, balconies and yards [bc.spa.sched.bylaw8]. Read it with standard bylaw 2 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw2].

## When it applies to your building

Standard bylaw 8 is one of your building's bylaws unless, and to the extent that, a different bylaw on this subject is filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120]. An owner developer may have filed different bylaws when the strata plan was deposited [bc.spa.s120], and owners may have amended them since by 3/4 vote and filing an Amendment to Bylaws; until that filing an amendment has no effect [bc.spa.s128]. So the question is always what the registered bylaws say, not what the standard bylaw says.

## What a building can change, and the limits

The Act sets the outer bounds. The corporation must repair and maintain common property and common assets; it may by bylaw shift limited common property the owner may use onto that owner, and other common property onto an owner only where the Regulation identifies it and subject to the prescribed restrictions; and it may take on specified portions of a strata lot [bc.spa.s72]. Taking on parts of some lots but not others changes the cost sharing [bc.spr.s6.5]. Amendments here are where most water leak and balcony disputes are won or lost, because they decide who pays before anyone argues about fault.

## What to check in the registered bylaws

Whether the registered bylaws keep the standard four tiers or redraw them; whether anything has been shifted to owners that the Regulation does not allow to be shifted; and whether standard bylaws 2 and 8 have been amended consistently, since a gap between them leaves an item with no one responsible.
