# Bylaw pattern: short-term rentals

## The shapes this bylaw takes

Three shapes recur. An outright prohibition on using all or part of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation. A minimum stay, expressed as a number of days or a month, sometimes tied to a tenancy agreement. And a use-based bylaw that does not mention letting at all but forbids commercial use, or use other than as a private residence. Buildings that mean to fine at the higher rate have to pair the prohibition with an amendment to the maximum fine and to the frequency of fines.

## What the Act and Regulation allow

The Regulation is where the specific authority shows itself: for a bylaw that prohibits or limits use of all or part of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation, the maximum fine is $1,000 for each contravention and a fine may be imposed daily rather than every 7 days [bc.spr.s7.1]. Those figures are ceilings the bylaws must claim: the corporation must set its maximum fine in its bylaws and cannot exceed the regulated maximum [bc.spa.s132], so a building that keeps the standard bylaws is limited to $50 and a fine every 7 days no matter what its short-term rental bylaw says [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spa.sched.bylaw24]. The open question is the other direction: section 141 forbids a corporation from otherwise restricting the rental of a strata lot [bc.spa.s141], and nothing in the Act or Regulation as imported draws the line between a rental that cannot be restricted and short-term accommodation that the Regulation assumes can be. That tension is open question 33 and is not resolved here. The Act and Regulation also say nothing about the provincial short-term rental registration scheme, which is a separate enactment not in the kb. A use-based bylaw relies on the general bylaw power [bc.spa.s119] and on the standard use bylaw [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3], and like any bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act or another enactment [bc.spa.s121].

## Where these bylaws go wrong

Fining at $1,000 under a bylaw that never set a maximum above the standard $50; fining daily when the bylaws still say every 7 days; and skipping the complaint and answer process because the contravention seems obvious from a listing — the corporation must still give written particulars and a reasonable opportunity to answer, including a hearing if requested, before it fines [bc.spa.s135]. Advertising is evidence of use, not use itself, and a bylaw that fines for advertising is a different bylaw from one that fines for the accommodation.
