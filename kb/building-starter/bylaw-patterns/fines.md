# Bylaw pattern: fines

## The shapes this bylaw takes

Four shapes. A single maximum, replacing the standard bylaw's figure with one number for every bylaw. A schedule of maximums, setting different figures for different bylaws, typically a high one for short-term accommodation and lower ones for the rest. A frequency bylaw, changing how often a continuing contravention may be fined. And an escalating scheme, where a first contravention draws a warning and later ones draw rising amounts. Standard bylaws 23 and 24 have to be amended together: raising the maximum in 23 and leaving 24 alone leaves a building able to fine $200 but still only once a week [bc.spa.sched.bylaw23, bc.spa.sched.bylaw24].

## What the Act and Regulation allow

The corporation must set its maximum fine in its bylaws, may set different maximums for different bylaws and rules and a frequency for continuing contraventions, and may not exceed the regulated maximums [bc.spa.s132]. Those maximums are $200 for each contravention of a bylaw, $50 for each contravention of a rule, and $1,000 for each contravention of a bylaw prohibiting or limiting use of a residential strata lot for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation; the frequency cap is every 7 days, and daily for that short-term accommodation bylaw [bc.spr.s7.1]. A fine is one of three enforcement options, alongside remedying a contravention and denying access to a recreational facility [bc.spa.s129, bc.spa.s130, bc.spa.s133, bc.spa.s134]. The procedure is the part an escalating scheme most often gets wrong: before imposing a fine, requiring payment of the costs of remedying a contravention, or denying a recreational facility, the corporation must have received a complaint, given the owner or tenant the particulars of the complaint in writing and a reasonable opportunity to answer including a hearing if requested, and where the person is a tenant given notice to the landlord and the owner; it must then give written notice of its decision as soon as feasible [bc.spa.s135]. A hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting [bc.spr.s7.2]. Once that has been done for a contravention, a continuing contravention of the same bylaw or rule may be fined without repeating it [bc.spa.s135]. A landlord and owner remain responsible for a tenant's fines [bc.spa.s131], and the decision to fine cannot be delegated [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20].

## Where these bylaws go wrong

Fining above the building's own bylaw maximum, or above the regulated cap; fining more often than the bylaws allow; treating a warning step in an escalating scheme as a substitute for the written particulars and opportunity to answer; letting the strata manager decide the fine rather than council [bc.spa.sched.bylaw20]; not notifying the landlord and owner when the person fined is a tenant; and not giving written notice of the decision [bc.spa.s135]. A bylaw that set a fine above the regulated maximum is unenforceable to the extent of the excess rather than wholly void [bc.spa.s121, bc.spa.s132].
