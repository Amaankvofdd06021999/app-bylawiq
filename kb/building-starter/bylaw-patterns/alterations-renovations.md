# Bylaw pattern: alterations and renovations

## The shapes this bylaw takes

These bylaws build on standard bylaws 5 and 6 [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5, bc.spa.sched.bylaw6] and take four shapes: a process bylaw (written application, drawings, permits, contractor insurance, a deposit, work hours); an indemnity or alteration agreement bylaw, requiring the owner to assume future repair, maintenance and insurance consequences of the alteration and often binding successors; a schedule-of-alterations bylaw, listing what needs approval, what needs none, and what is prohibited; and a responsibility-shifting bylaw, moving repair of an altered element permanently onto the owner.

## What the Act and Regulation allow

Approval and responsibility are different things, and the Act treats them differently. Standard bylaw 5 already says approval for a strata lot alteration must not be unreasonably withheld, and allows a condition that the owner take responsibility in writing for the expenses of the alteration [bc.spa.sched.bylaw5]; standard bylaw 6, for common property, has no equivalent duty not to withhold [bc.spa.sched.bylaw6]. Shifting the *ongoing* duty to repair is bounded: the corporation may by bylaw make an owner responsible for limited common property the owner may use, and for other common property only where the Regulation identifies it and subject to the prescribed restrictions, and it may take on specified portions of a strata lot; where it takes on portions of some lots but not others, the cost is shared by a different formula [bc.spa.s72, bc.spr.s6.5, bc.spa.sched.bylaw8]. Council cannot approve, under an alterations bylaw, something that is really a significant change in the use or appearance of common property, which needs the owners' vote [bc.spa.s71]. An owner request to install EV charging infrastructure on common property has its own statutory route [bc.spa.s90.1, bc.spa.s90.2]. And an alteration to a part of the lot the corporation must insure engages the insurance duty whatever the bylaw says [bc.spa.s149].

## Where these bylaws go wrong

Using an alteration agreement to move a repair obligation the Act does not allow to be moved; approving at council level a change that needed a 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s71]; withholding approval for a strata lot alteration without a reason, when the standard bylaw requires that approval not be unreasonably withheld; relying on an alteration agreement against a later owner without checking whether it binds successors; and treating an unapproved alteration as something to fine for without the complaint and answer process [bc.spa.s135]. Removing an unapproved alteration is a remedy with its own requirements, not a self-help step.
