# Bylaw pattern: pets

## The shapes this bylaw takes

A pet bylaw works by replacing the standard use bylaw's pet allowance [bc.spa.sched.bylaw3], and takes one of four shapes: a complete prohibition; a numerical limit (one pet, or one dog or cat); a type or size limit, sometimes by weight or breed; and a registration and conduct scheme, where pets are allowed but must be registered, leashed, carried in elevators or kept off lawns. Many buildings combine a limit with a conduct scheme, and some add a power to require removal of a pet that is a nuisance.

## What the Act and Regulation allow

Two statutory limits cannot be drafted around. A bylaw that prohibits a pet does not apply to a pet that was already living with an owner, tenant or occupant immediately before the bylaw passed, if living with the pet did not contravene a pet bylaw then and the pet keeps living there [bc.spa.s123]. This is why the date a pet bylaw was filed matters as much as its words. And a bylaw that prohibits or restricts an animal, or its access to a strata lot or common property, never applies to a guide dog or service dog, or to the dog of a retired guide or service dog team where the handler is an owner, tenant or occupant [bc.spa.s123]. The definitions come from the *Guide Dog and Service Dog Act*, which the kb does not hold (open question 32). Beyond those, a bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the *Human Rights Code* [bc.spa.s121], which is the route by which a request for an assistance animal that is not a guide or service dog is assessed. The general bylaw power supports the rest [bc.spa.s119], and amendments need a 3/4 vote and filing [bc.spa.s128].

## Where these bylaws go wrong

Applying a new limit to a pet that was lawfully there before it passed; treating a grandfathered pet as a precedent for a replacement pet (the protection attaches to that pet, for as long as it keeps living there); asking for proof of a disability rather than of the animal's status where a guide or service dog is involved; and fining without first giving written particulars of the complaint and a reasonable opportunity to answer, including a hearing if requested [bc.spa.s135].
