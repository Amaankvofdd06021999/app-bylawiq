# Documents to upload for a new building

BylawIQ answers from the building's own documents first, and from BC strata law second. The law is the same for every building; the documents are what make an answer about *this* building. The more of this list a building uploads, the fewer questions end in "no grounding found".

Nothing here is a document a building has to create. Every item is something the strata corporation already has to prepare or retain [bc.spa.s35], and must make available to an owner on request [bc.spa.s36]. If a building cannot find one of these, that gap is itself worth knowing about.

## How to read this list

Each entry says why an answer needs the document, which topics it unlocks, and the provision that requires the strata corporation to have it. "Unlocks" means questions on that topic can be answered for this building rather than in general terms.

## Registered bylaws, with every filed amendment — essential

**Why an answer needs it.** This is the single most important upload. A building's bylaws are the Standard Bylaws only to the extent that different bylaws have not been filed in the land title office [bc.spa.s120], and an amendment has no effect until it is filed [bc.spa.s128]. So an answer about what the rules are is only as good as the filed set. Upload the consolidated bylaws *and* each amendment as filed, because the consolidation can be out of date or wrong, and the filing date decides which version governed a past event.

**Topics it unlocks.** Nearly all of them, and in particular `pets`, `noise`, `nuisance`, `rentals`, `short-term-rentals`, `parking`, `smoking-cannabis`, `alterations-renovations`, `fines`, `bylaw-enforcement-s135` and `repair-maintenance`.

**Where the law requires it.** The strata corporation must have bylaws [bc.spa.s119] and must retain the bylaws and rules [bc.spa.s35], keeping current copies [bc.spr.s4.1].

## Rules made by council — essential

**Why an answer needs it.** Rules are not bylaws. They can only govern the use, safety and condition of common property and common assets; a bylaw prevails over a conflicting rule; and a new rule stops having effect at the first annual general meeting after it is made unless it is ratified [bc.spa.s125]. So a rule that was never ratified may not be enforceable, and BylawIQ cannot see that without the rules and the minutes that ratified them. Fines for contravening a rule also have a lower maximum than fines for a bylaw.

**Topics it unlocks.** `common-property-use`, `parking`, `move-in-move-out`, `fines`.

**Where the law requires it.** Rules must be set out in a written document capable of being photocopied, and owners and tenants must be told of new rules as soon as feasible [bc.spa.s125]; the corporation must retain them [bc.spa.s35].

## Strata plan — essential

**Why an answer needs it.** The plan is what decides whether a thing is a strata lot, common property, limited common property or a common asset, and that single distinction drives who repairs it, who insures it, who pays and who votes. Without the plan, answers about balconies, patios, parking, storage and water leaks stay general.

**Topics it unlocks.** `limited-common-property`, `common-property-use`, `repair-maintenance`, `water-leaks`, `parking`, `insurance-deductibles`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must retain the registered strata plan and any amendments as obtained from the land title office [bc.spa.s35], permanently [bc.spr.s4.1].

## Minutes: general meetings and council meetings — essential

**Why an answer needs it.** Minutes are where the building's decisions live: bylaw amendments passed, rules ratified, special levies approved, budgets adopted, councils elected, and — critically for enforcement — the council decisions on complaints and fines. A question about whether a fine was properly imposed is answered from the minutes, not from the bylaws.

**Topics it unlocks.** `general-meetings-voting`, `council-governance`, `bylaw-enforcement-s135`, `fines`, `special-levies`, `contingency-reserve-fund`, `hearings`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must prepare minutes of annual and special general meetings and of council meetings, including the results of any votes [bc.spa.s35], must retain them for at least 6 years [bc.spr.s4.1], and under the standard bylaws council must inform owners of council minutes within two weeks of the meeting whether or not they have been approved [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19].

## Budget and financial statements — strongly recommended

**Why an answer needs it.** The budget is how strata fees are set, so almost any question about what an owner owes, or why fees went up, runs through it. It also shows what the corporation is and is not funding.

**Topics it unlocks.** `strata-fees-arrears`, `contingency-reserve-fund`, `special-levies`, `repair-maintenance`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must prepare a budget for approval by majority vote at each annual general meeting, distributed with the notice of that meeting and accompanied by a financial statement [bc.spa.s103], and must retain the budget and financial statement for the current and previous years [bc.spa.s35] for at least 6 years [bc.spr.s4.1].

## Depreciation report — strongly recommended

**Why an answer needs it.** It is the building's own estimate of what its major items will cost and when, so it grounds every question about whether the contingency reserve fund is adequate, whether a special levy is coming, and what the corporation already knew about a failing component.

**Topics it unlocks.** `depreciation-report`, `contingency-reserve-fund`, `special-levies`, `repair-maintenance`.

**Where the law requires it.** A strata corporation must obtain a depreciation report from a qualified person by the dates the Regulation sets, containing the information the Regulation sets [bc.spa.s94, bc.spr.s6.2], and must retain any it has obtained [bc.spa.s35], permanently [bc.spr.s4.1]. A new report is required at least every 5 years. A corporation established before 1 July 2024 that had not obtained one since 31 December 2020 had to obtain one before 1 July 2026 if it is wholly or partly in the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District or the Metro Vancouver Regional District (excluding islands reachable only by air or boat), and otherwise before 1 July 2027; newer corporations have their own deadlines running from the first annual general meeting [bc.spr.s6.21]. The requirement does not apply while a strata plan has fewer than 5 strata lots [bc.spr.s6.22]. If a building has no report, upload the resolution or the exemption reason instead, because "there is no report" is an answer that needs a source too.

## Insurance summary, and the policy if available — strongly recommended

**Why an answer needs it.** Who pays for water damage usually turns on the deductible and on what the policy covers, not on who was careless. The deductible amount is the number most owner questions about a leak actually need.

**Topics it unlocks.** `insurance-deductibles`, `water-leaks`, `repair-maintenance`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must obtain and maintain property insurance on the property the Act lists [bc.spa.s149], must review the adequacy of its insurance annually, report on coverage at each annual general meeting, and inform owners and tenants as soon as feasible of any material change including an increase in a deductible [bc.spa.s154]. Insurance policies are written contracts the corporation must retain, for at least 6 years after the policy ends [bc.spa.s35, bc.spr.s4.1].

## Information Certificate — useful

**Why an answer needs it.** It is a snapshot of one strata lot's position: monthly strata fees, what the owner owes, alteration agreements the owner has signed, approved special levies still to pay, an expected budget overrun, the contingency reserve fund balance net of approved spending, unfiled bylaw amendments and unfiled resolutions, notices given for votes not yet held, proceedings and judgments against the corporation, and outstanding work orders [bc.spa.s59]. Uploading recent certificates gives BylawIQ a dated record of several of those facts at once.

**Topics it unlocks.** `strata-fees-arrears`, `special-levies`, `bylaw-amendment-filing`, `contingency-reserve-fund`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must give an Information Certificate in the prescribed form within one week of a request by an owner, a purchaser or someone authorised by either [bc.spa.s59], and must retain the certificates it issues [bc.spa.s35] for at least 6 years [bc.spr.s4.1]. Note on naming: this document is widely called "Form B". The kb does not hold the Regulation's Schedule of Forms, so it cannot confirm that letter; the Act calls it an Information Certificate, and so does BylawIQ. See open question 23.

## Electrical planning report — essential where it is due

**Why an answer needs it.** It is the only record of what the building's electrical system can carry,
and it is now a statutory requirement with a deadline. It also decides when owners may start asking
to install EV charging, because the report date is the gate on those requests [bc.spr.s5.4].

**Topics it unlocks.** `electrical-planning-reports`, `ev-charging`, `repair-maintenance`,
`contingency-reserve-fund`.

**Where the law requires it.** A strata corporation must obtain an electrical planning report from a
qualified person by the dates the Regulation sets [bc.spa.s94.1, bc.spr.s5.10], and must retain any
it obtains [bc.spa.s35] permanently [bc.spr.s4.1]. For a corporation established on or before
31 December 2023 with a plan that is not phased, the date is 31 December 2026 where the land is
wholly or partly in the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District or the Metro
Vancouver Regional District, and 31 December 2028 otherwise; a plan with fewer than 5 strata lots on
31 December 2023 is exempt [bc.spr.s5.8, bc.spr.s5.7]. If the building has no report yet, upload
what establishes which group it is in — the strata plan, the establishment date and the lot count —
so the deadline can be worked out.

## Engineering and other major-item reports — useful

**Why an answer needs it.** These are what the corporation knew, and when. A question about whether the corporation acted reasonably on a building envelope or plumbing problem is answered from the reports and the minutes that responded to them.

**Topics it unlocks.** `repair-maintenance`, `water-leaks`, `depreciation-report`, `special-levies`.

**Where the law requires it.** The corporation must retain reports it obtained respecting repair or maintenance of major items, including engineers' reports, risk management reports and sanitation reports, until the items they relate to are disposed of or replaced, and must permanently retain electrical planning reports and reports about EV charging infrastructure [bc.spa.s35, bc.spa.s94.1, bc.spr.s4.1].

## Strata management contract and major service contracts — useful

**Why an answer needs it.** The management contract sets out what the manager may do on council's behalf, which matters because some enforcement decisions cannot be delegated at all. Service contracts show who is responsible for work an owner is complaining about.

**Topics it unlocks.** `council-governance`, `repair-maintenance`, `bylaw-enforcement-s135`.

**Where the law requires it.** Written contracts to which the corporation is a party are records it must retain, for at least 6 years after the contract ends [bc.spa.s35, bc.spr.s4.1].

## Documents received from the owner developer — useful, and often missed

**Why an answer needs it.** A newer building's warranties, as-built drawings, permits and original contracts came across at the first annual general meeting and answer maintenance questions nothing else can.

**Topics it unlocks.** `repair-maintenance`, `depreciation-report`, `insurance-deductibles`.

**Where the law requires it.** The owner developer must hand over the listed records and documents at the first annual general meeting [bc.spa.s20], and the corporation must retain them, with retention periods set per document including permanent retention for several and warranty-linked periods for others [bc.spa.s35, bc.spr.s4.1].

## Past enforcement correspondence — useful

**Why an answer needs it.** A fine is usually challenged on process, not on the facts, so what was sent and when decides the answer. Upload the complaint letters, the notices, hearing requests and decision letters.

**Topics it unlocks.** `bylaw-enforcement-s135`, `fines`, `hearings`, `harassment`.

**Where the law requires it.** Correspondence sent or received by the corporation and council is a record it must retain, for at least 2 years [bc.spa.s35, bc.spr.s4.1]. Redact it as the building's privacy policy requires before uploading; it names residents.

## What BylawIQ cannot answer without documents

With no uploads, BylawIQ can still say what the Act, the Regulation and the standard bylaws require, and what a building's options are. It cannot say what *this* building's bylaws say, whether a rule was ratified, who is responsible for a particular balcony, what the insurance deductible is, or whether a fine followed the required process. Those answers should come back as "no grounding found" rather than as a guess, and the eval set is written to check that they do.

## A note on retention periods

Retention periods are worth knowing when a building says a document no longer exists. They are set by the Regulation against the list of records in the Act [bc.spa.s35, bc.spr.s4.1].

- **Permanently:** the registered strata plan and amendments; resolutions dealing with changes to common property, including the designation of limited common property; any decision of an arbitrator, judge or the Civil Resolution Tribunal in a proceeding the corporation was party to, and any legal opinions it obtained; depreciation reports; electrical planning reports; and reports about the installation or operation of EV charging infrastructure.
- **Until the item is disposed of or replaced:** reports about the repair or maintenance of major items, such as engineers', risk management and sanitation reports.
- **At least 6 years:** minutes of general and council meetings; books of account; waivers and consents under sections 41, 44 and 45; budgets and financial statements; income tax returns; bank statements, cancelled cheques and certificates of deposit; and Information Certificates. Written contracts, including insurance policies, must be kept for at least 6 years after the contract or policy ends.
- **At least 2 years:** correspondence sent or received by the corporation and council.
- **Current copies only:** the list of council members; the list of owners, of mortgagees who have filed a Mortgagee's Request for Notification, of tenants, and of landlord-to-tenant assignments; the Act and the regulations; the bylaws and rules; and a phone number or other way to reach each council member at short notice.
- **Documents from the owner developer** have their own schedule, from permanent retention for several of them to periods tied to a warranty's expiry.

So the bylaws and rules are the one essential upload the corporation is not required to keep a history of — only current copies. That is why every filed amendment matters: the corporation may have no obligation to have kept the superseded versions, and the land title office is the record of what was filed and when.
