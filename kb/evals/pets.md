# Evals for pets

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Pet allowance where the building has filed no pet bylaw

- **Question:** Our bylaws do not mention pets. How many pets can an owner keep?
- **Scope:** a building whose registered bylaws contain no pet bylaw.
- **Expected points:** The standard bylaws are the building's bylaws except where a different bylaw is filed, so the standard allowance applies: a reasonable number of fish or other small aquarium animals, a reasonable number of small caged mammals, up to 2 caged birds, and one dog or one cat.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s120`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** A limit taken from some other building's bylaws; that pets are unrestricted because the bylaws are silent.

## Case 2: A new no-pets bylaw and a cat that was already there

- **Question:** We just passed a no-pets bylaw. An owner already has a cat. Do they have to remove it?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A bylaw prohibiting a pet does not apply to a pet that was living with the owner, tenant or occupant immediately before the bylaw passed, if keeping it contravened no pet bylaw then, and it goes on living with them. The protection attaches to that pet, so it does not carry over to a replacement pet.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123`
- **Must not say:** That the cat must be removed; that filing the bylaw overrides the exemption; that the exemption covers any future pet.

## Case 3: Guide dog where the bylaws prohibit pets

- **Question:** A new tenant has a guide dog and our bylaws say no pets. Can we enforce the bylaw?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A bylaw prohibiting or restricting a pet or other animal, or its access to a strata lot or common property, does not apply to a guide dog or service dog, or to a dog of a retired guide or service dog team where the team member is an owner, tenant or occupant. The answer should note that those terms are defined in the *Guide Dog and Service Dog Act*, which the kb does not hold.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123`
- **Must not say:** That the bylaw can be enforced; that the tenant must prove a disability; a definition of "guide dog" or "service dog" stated as though the kb contained it.

## Case 4: Maximum fine for a pet contravention under the standard bylaws

- **Question:** What is the most we can fine for breaking the pet bylaw?
- **Scope:** a building that has not amended the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** $50 for each contravention of a bylaw, because that is what the standard bylaw sets and the corporation can only fine up to the maximum in its own bylaws. The Regulation would allow up to $200, but only if the building amends its bylaws to say so.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw23`, `bc.spa.s132`, `bc.spr.s7.1`
- **Must not say:** $200, as though the regulated maximum applied automatically.

## Case 5: An animal that is not a guide or service dog

- **Question:** An owner says their dog is an emotional support animal and our pet bylaw bans dogs. What do we do?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The statutory exemption covers guide dogs, service dogs and retired guide or service dog team dogs, and does not by its terms extend further. Separately, a bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the *Human Rights Code*. The answer should say the kb does not hold the Code, so it cannot state the test, and that this needs advice.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123`, `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** The Human Rights Code test for accommodation or undue hardship, stated as though it were in the kb; a flat yes or no.

## Case 6: No grounding: a weight limit

- **Question:** What is the maximum weight for a dog in this building?
- **Scope:** a building whose uploaded documents set no weight limit.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer says the building's documents set no weight limit and does not supply one.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any weight in pounds or kilograms; a limit from the standard bylaws, which set none.
