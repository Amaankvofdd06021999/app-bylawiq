# Evals for water leaks

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Who repairs a leaking balcony

- **Question:** Water is coming through a balcony above. Who has to fix it?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws, where the balcony is limited common property.
- **Expected points:** Under the standard bylaws the corporation repairs and maintains limited common property for work that in the ordinary course happens less often than once a year, and for a listed set of building elements however often they need work, including balconies and things attached to the exterior. The owner's duty covers the rest of the limited common property they use. The strata plan decides what the balcony is.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw8`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw2`, `bc.spa.s72`
- **Must not say:** That the owner is responsible simply because only they use the balcony.

## Case 2: Charging the deductible to an owner

- **Question:** A leak from a unit caused a claim. Can we just charge the $5,000 deductible to that owner?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not automatically. Paying the deductible on a claim on the corporation's insurance is a common expense contributed to through strata fees. The corporation may sue the owner to recover it where the owner is responsible for the loss or damage, and a building's own bylaw may create a chargeback, but responsibility is something that has to be established.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s158`
- **Must not say:** That the Act makes the owner liable for the deductible; that a chargeback needs no basis.

## Case 3: Whether an owner is covered by the corporation's policy

- **Question:** Is the owner insured under our policy?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Named insureds include the corporation, the owners and tenants from time to time, and the persons who normally occupy the strata lots, whatever the policy says.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s155`
- **Must not say:** That only the corporation is insured; a statement about what the policy covers, which the kb cannot know.

## Case 4: Getting into the unit

- **Question:** The owner will not let our plumber in. What can we do?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** The standard bylaw requires entry in an emergency without notice to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage, and otherwise at a reasonable time on 48 hours' written notice, for work the corporation is responsible for; the notice must give the date, approximate time and reason. If entry is still refused, the route is enforcement and, if needed, an order.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw7`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation may enter without notice outside an emergency; that a locksmith may be used as self-help.

## Case 5: Paying the deductible without a vote

- **Question:** Do owners have to approve a levy to pay the deductible?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. Approval is not required for a special levy or a contingency reserve fund expenditure to cover an insurance deductible the corporation must pay to repair or replace damaged property, unless the corporation has decided not to repair or replace.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s158`
- **Must not say:** That a 3/4 vote is needed; that the reserve fund cannot be used.

## Case 6: No grounding: the deductible amount

- **Question:** What is our water damage deductible?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no insurance summary.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer says the deductible is set by the building's policy and that the policy or summary is not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any dollar figure; a typical or industry-standard deductible.
