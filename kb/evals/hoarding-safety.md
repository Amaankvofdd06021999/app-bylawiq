# Evals for hoarding and safety

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Getting in

- **Question:** We think there is a fire hazard in a unit. Can we enter?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** In an emergency, without notice, to ensure safety or prevent significant loss or damage. Otherwise at a reasonable time on 48 hours' written notice giving the date, approximate time and reason, and only for work the corporation is responsible for.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw7`
- **Must not say:** That a suspicion of hoarding is itself an emergency; that the corporation may enter at will.

## Case 2: Clearing the unit

- **Question:** Can we hire someone to clear the unit and charge the owner?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The corporation may do what is reasonably necessary to remedy a contravention of its bylaws or rules and require the reasonable costs from the person who could be fined, but only after the complaint and answer process.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s133`, `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation may clear a unit as self-help.

## Case 3: A fire department order

- **Question:** The fire department served an order about a unit. What is the sequence?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Promptly give the notice or order to the owner, who must do the work. If they do not, the corporation may do it, must wait if told in writing of an appeal, and except in an emergency must give a week's written notice of its intention.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s84`, `bc.spa.s85`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation can act immediately; that it may ignore an appeal.

## Case 4: Accommodation

- **Question:** The owner has a mental health condition. Does that change anything?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Human Rights Code, so enforcement may be constrained. The Code is not in the kb, so the answer must not state the test, and should say this needs advice and that enforcement should not run while the question is unresolved.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** The accommodation test; a conclusion that enforcement may simply proceed.

## Case 5: No grounding: is this a hazard

- **Question:** Is the state of unit 3 a fire hazard?
- **Scope:** a building with no uploaded inspection report.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** A conclusion based on a description alone.
