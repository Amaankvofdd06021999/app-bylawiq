# Evals for strata fees and arrears

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Charging interest on late fees

- **Question:** Can we charge interest on overdue strata fees?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** Not as things stand. Interest exists only where a bylaw that establishes a payment schedule sets a rate, capped at 10% per annum compounded annually. The standard bylaw sets a due date and no interest, so a building that has not amended it has no interest to charge.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s107`, `bc.spr.s6.8`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw1`
- **Must not say:** A rate above the cap; interest as an automatic entitlement; interest described as a fine.

## Case 2: Fining an owner for not paying

- **Question:** Can we fine an owner who is three months behind on fees?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Unpaid fees are a debt, not a bylaw contravention, so the fine machinery is not the route; the Act's collection sequence is. If the building has a bylaw that the arrears themselves contravene, the answer should say the enforcement process would still have to be followed.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s112`, `bc.spa.s116`, `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That arrears can simply be fined; that a fine and a lien are alternatives for the same debt.

## Case 3: Notice before a lien

- **Question:** How much notice before we register a lien?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** At least two weeks' written notice demanding payment and saying that a lien may be registered if payment is not made within that period. A separate two weeks' notice is required before suing, starting arbitration, or a tribunal initiating notice in respect of collection.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s112`
- **Must not say:** Any period other than two weeks; that the same notice serves both purposes without saying so.

## Case 4: What can go on a lien

- **Question:** Can we add our lawyer's bill to the lien?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Reasonable legal costs, land title and court registry fees and other reasonable disbursements of registering or enforcing the lien may be added to the amount owing under the Certificate of Lien. The lien itself covers strata fees, a special levy, reimbursement of the cost of work under section 85, and the lot's share of a judgment against the corporation.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s118`, `bc.spa.s116`
- **Must not say:** That fines may be secured by the lien; that any legal cost may be added regardless of reasonableness.

## Case 5: After the lien

- **Question:** The lien is registered and they still are not paying. Can we sell the unit?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not on the corporation's own authority. After registering a Certificate of Lien it may apply to the Supreme Court for an order for sale, and the court decides after considering all the circumstances.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s117`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation may sell the strata lot itself; that sale follows automatically from the lien.

## Case 6: No grounding: the balance owing

- **Question:** How much does unit 7 owe?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no ledger or financial records.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer says the amount comes from the building's own accounts and those are not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any figure; an estimate from the strata fee amount.
