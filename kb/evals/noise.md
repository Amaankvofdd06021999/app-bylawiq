# Evals for noise

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: A rule about noise inside a strata lot

- **Question:** Can council make a rule banning hard flooring noise inside units?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. Rules may only govern the use, safety and condition of common property and common assets, so a rule cannot reach conduct inside a strata lot. That would need a bylaw, passed by 3/4 vote and filed.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s125`, `bc.spa.s128`
- **Must not say:** That a rule can regulate the interior of a strata lot; that a rule and a bylaw are interchangeable.

## Case 2: A rule that was never ratified

- **Question:** We made a quiet hours rule for the gym 18 months ago and never put it to a vote. Can we enforce it?
- **Scope:** a building that has held an annual general meeting since the rule was made.
- **Expected points:** No. A rule ceases to have effect at the first annual general meeting after it is made unless ratified by majority vote at that meeting or at a special general meeting before it.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s125`
- **Must not say:** That a rule stays in force until repealed; that posting the rule is enough.

## Case 3: No quiet hours in the bylaws

- **Question:** Our bylaws have no quiet hours. Does that mean night-time noise is allowed?
- **Scope:** a building with only the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** No. The standard use bylaw forbids unreasonable noise and unreasonable interference with others' use and enjoyment at any hour; quiet hours are an addition some buildings make, not the source of the obligation.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`, `bc.spa.s120`
- **Must not say:** That noise is permitted outside stated quiet hours; a specific time window the building has not adopted.

## Case 4: Fining on a first complaint

- **Question:** We got one noise complaint with a video. Can we fine right away?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not without the process first: a complaint, written particulars given to the owner or tenant, a reasonable opportunity to answer including a hearing if requested, notice to a tenant's landlord and owner, and then written notice of the decision. The strength of the evidence does not remove the steps.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That clear evidence allows an immediate fine; that a warning letter is the same as written particulars.

## Case 5: Replacing carpet with hard flooring

- **Question:** An owner wants to replace carpet with laminate. Do they need permission?
- **Scope:** a building with only the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** It depends on what the alteration touches. The standard bylaw requires written approval before altering listed parts of a strata lot, and the answer should set out that list and note that a building's own bylaw may add a flooring requirement. The noise obligation under the use bylaw applies regardless.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw5`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** A flat yes or no without reference to what the alteration involves; an underlay rating the building has not adopted.

## Case 6: No grounding: a decibel limit

- **Question:** What decibel level counts as unreasonable noise here?
- **Scope:** a building whose documents set no measurable standard.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state for the number. The answer says the standard is unreasonableness, not a measured level, and that the building's documents set no decibel limit.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** Any decibel figure; a municipal noise bylaw limit, which the kb does not hold.
