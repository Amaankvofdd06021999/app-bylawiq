# Evals for bylaw enforcement and s.135

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: The order of the steps

- **Question:** What do we have to do before fining an owner?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Receive a complaint about the contravention; give the owner the particulars of the complaint in writing; give a reasonable opportunity to answer it, including a hearing if the owner requests one; and, where the person is a tenant, notify the landlord and the owner. After deciding, give written notice of the decision as soon as feasible.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That a warning letter satisfies the particulars requirement; that the decision may be communicated orally.

## Case 2: What a hearing means

- **Question:** The owner asked for a hearing. Can we deal with it by email?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s7.2`, `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That a written exchange satisfies a request for a hearing.

## Case 3: Which penalties the process covers

- **Question:** Does section 135 apply if we are only charging the cost of a repair?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Yes, where the charge is requiring a person to pay the costs of remedying a contravention. The section covers three things: imposing a fine, requiring payment of the costs of remedying a contravention, and denying the use of a recreational facility.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`, `bc.spa.s129`, `bc.spa.s134`
- **Must not say:** That the process applies only to fines; that a chargeback is outside it when it is a cost of remedying a contravention.

## Case 4: Deciding at the same meeting as the hearing

- **Question:** Can council decide the fine at the end of the hearing?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The Act does not forbid deciding after hearing the person; what it requires is that the opportunity to answer comes before the decision, and that written notice of the decision follows as soon as feasible. The answer should not go further than that.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** A rule that council must reserve its decision, or that it must decide immediately; either would be invented.

## Case 5: Who may decide

- **Question:** Council is short-handed. Can the president decide the fines alone?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** No. The decision whether a person contravened, whether to fine and how much cannot be delegated, including to a single council member. Council decides.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw20`
- **Must not say:** That the president or a committee may decide alone under a delegation.

## Case 6: No grounding: did we comply?

- **Question:** Did we follow section 135 for the complaint we sent in March?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no correspondence or council minutes.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer lists what compliance requires and says the records needed to check are not available.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** A finding that the process was or was not followed.
