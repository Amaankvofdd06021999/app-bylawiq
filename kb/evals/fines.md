# Evals for fines

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Maximum fine for breaking a rule

- **Question:** What is the most we can fine for a rule contravention?
- **Scope:** a building that has not amended the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** $10, because that is the standard bylaw's maximum for a rule. The Regulation would allow up to $50, but only if the building's bylaws say so.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw23`, `bc.spa.s132`, `bc.spr.s7.1`
- **Must not say:** $50 as though the regulated maximum applied automatically; the bylaw figure of $200, which is for bylaw contraventions.

## Case 2: A bylaw setting a higher fine than the Regulation allows

- **Question:** Our bylaw says $500 per contravention for any bylaw. Is the bylaw void?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not wholly. A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act or regulations, so it is ineffective as to the excess over the regulated maximum, which is $200 for an ordinary bylaw contravention.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`, `bc.spa.s132`, `bc.spr.s7.1`
- **Must not say:** That the whole bylaw is void; that $500 can be charged.

## Case 3: The strata manager imposing fines

- **Question:** Can our property manager issue the fines to save council time?
- **Scope:** any building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** No. Council may delegate widely, but not the decisions whether a person contravened a bylaw or rule, whether to fine and how much, or whether to deny a recreational facility. The manager may prepare and send the correspondence; the decision has to be council's and recorded as such.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw20`
- **Must not say:** That a delegation resolution can cover the decision to fine.

## Case 4: Repeating the process for a continuing contravention

- **Question:** Do we have to send fresh particulars every week while the breach continues?
- **Scope:** a building that has already completed the process once for that contravention.
- **Expected points:** No. Once the corporation has complied with the complaint and answer requirements for a contravention of a bylaw or rule, it may fine for a continuing contravention of that same bylaw or rule without complying again. That applies to the same bylaw, not to a different one, and to a contravention that continues rather than a fresh one after a gap.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw24`
- **Must not say:** That the shortcut applies to any later contravention; that it removes the frequency limit.

## Case 5: Denying the gym instead of fining

- **Question:** Instead of a fine, can we just suspend gym access?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** That is one of the three enforcement options, but the same process applies before it: complaint, written particulars, a reasonable opportunity to answer including a hearing if requested, and written notice of the decision. It may be for a reasonable length of time and must relate to a contravention of a bylaw or rule about that facility.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s129`, `bc.spa.s134`, `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That denying a facility avoids the section 135 process; an indefinite suspension.

## Case 6: No grounding: was this fine valid?

- **Question:** Was the $200 fine on unit 12 properly imposed?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no enforcement correspondence or minutes.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer sets out what the process requires and says the building's records are not available to check whether it was followed.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** A conclusion that the fine was or was not valid.
