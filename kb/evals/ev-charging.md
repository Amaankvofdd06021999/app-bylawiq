# Evals for EV charging

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: how long council has to decide

- **Question:** An owner has asked to install a charger at their stall. How long do we have?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The corporation must decide whether to approve within the prescribed period after receiving the request, which is 3 months, and must not unreasonably refuse.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s90.2`, `bc.spr.s5.6`
- **Must not say:** Any period other than 3 months; that council may defer indefinitely pending a study.

## Case 2: refusing on capacity

- **Question:** Our building is old and we think the panel cannot take it. Can we just say no?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Capacity and current and anticipated demands are matters the corporation may consider, but it must not unreasonably refuse, and the electrical planning report is where capacity is established. The answer should not conclude that refusal is or is not reasonable here.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s90.2`, `bc.spa.s94.1`
- **Must not say:** That capacity is an automatic answer; a conclusion that this refusal would be reasonable.

## Case 3: the vote

- **Question:** Installing the conduit changes the look of the parkade. Do we need a 3/4 vote?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** A significant change in the use or appearance of common property normally needs a 3/4 vote, but where the change relates to installing EV charging infrastructure or managing the electricity it uses, a majority vote is enough.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s71`
- **Must not say:** 3/4 for the EV change; that no vote is ever needed.

## Case 4: how long the stall is theirs

- **Question:** The stall is common property. If we approve the charger, how long can the owner keep the stall?
- **Scope:** a stall that is common property and not limited common property.
- **Expected points:** Exclusive use of common property normally runs a year at a time, but a stall where EV charging infrastructure was installed following an owner's request under section 90.1 is a prescribed class with a period of 5 years.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s76`, `bc.spr.s5.101`
- **Must not say:** One year; that the stall becomes the owner's permanently.

## Case 5: who pays

- **Question:** If we approve, who pays for the work?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The owner is responsible for the expenses associated with making the alteration unless otherwise agreed. The corporation may make the alteration itself or let the owner do it; if it does the work, the owner pays in advance an amount the corporation considers sufficient and tops it up if it falls short.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s90.3`
- **Must not say:** That it is a common expense; that the corporation must fund it.

## Case 6: no grounding — the provincial rebate

- **Question:** What rebate can the owner get for the charger?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The kb holds the Strata Property Act and Regulation, which say nothing about rebates or funding programmes.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any rebate amount, programme name or eligibility rule.
