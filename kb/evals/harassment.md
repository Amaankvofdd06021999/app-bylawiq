# Evals for harassment

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Is there a harassment section

- **Question:** What does the Strata Property Act say about harassment?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Nothing directly. A harassment complaint is brought under the building's bylaws, and where nothing else is filed, under the standard use bylaw's nuisance, hazard and unreasonable interference limbs.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** A harassment section of the Act; a definition of harassment presented as statutory.

## Case 2: A complaint about a council member

- **Question:** An owner complains that a council member is harassing them. Can that member vote on it?
- **Scope:** a building where not all owners are on council.
- **Expected points:** No. If a complaint is made about a council member contravening a bylaw or rule, that member must not participate in the decision about the complaint.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s136`
- **Must not say:** That the member may participate if they declare an interest.

## Case 3: Complaints about council's conduct

- **Question:** Owners say council is bullying them. Which bylaw applies?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Conduct by council in exercising the corporation's powers is measured against the statutory standard of care — honesty, good faith, the best interests of the corporation, and the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person — rather than against a use bylaw.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s31`
- **Must not say:** That the use bylaw governs council's own conduct; a finding about the council's behaviour.

## Case 4: Protected grounds

- **Question:** The conduct complained of is about someone's religion. Anything different?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Human Rights Code, and the Code is not in the kb, so the answer must not state the test and should say the matter needs advice. Conduct that may be criminal is a matter for the police.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** The Human Rights Code test; advice to handle it internally.

## Case 5: No grounding: was this harassment

- **Question:** Were these emails harassment?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state as to the conclusion. The answer can set out which bylaw would apply and must say the kb holds no decisions and cannot say how the test has been applied.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** A finding either way.
