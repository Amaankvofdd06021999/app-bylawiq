# Evals for records and privacy

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: How long to answer a records request

- **Question:** An owner asked for two years of council minutes. How long do we have?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Two weeks. The one-week deadline applies only where the request is for the bylaws or rules.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s36`
- **Must not say:** Any period other than two weeks for minutes; the one-week deadline applied generally.

## Case 2: Asking why

- **Question:** Can we ask what the owner wants the records for before providing them?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The Act imposes no requirement that a requester give a reason, and access does not depend on one.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s36`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation may refuse until a reason is given; that council may weigh the purpose.

## Case 3: A former owner asking for records

- **Question:** Someone who sold last year wants records. Do we have to provide them?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Yes, for records relating to the period during which they were an owner, whenever those records were created, on a request from them or from someone they authorise in writing.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s36`
- **Must not say:** That the right ends on sale; that only current owners have access.

## Case 4: Circulating minutes before approval

- **Question:** Can we wait until the next meeting approves the minutes before sending them out?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** No. Council must inform owners of the minutes of every council meeting within two weeks of the meeting, whether or not the minutes have been approved.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw19`
- **Must not say:** That unapproved minutes may be withheld; any period other than two weeks.

## Case 5: A record that is not on the list

- **Question:** An owner wants the council's private email thread about a complaint. Must we produce it?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The right of access runs to the records the Act lists, which include correspondence sent or received by the corporation and council. The answer should set out that the list is the boundary in both directions, and should not decide whether a particular thread falls inside it.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s35`, `bc.spa.s36`
- **Must not say:** A confident yes or no about the particular thread; a privacy exemption from a statute the kb does not hold.

## Case 6: No grounding: what privacy law allows

- **Question:** Does privacy law let us publish the arrears list in the lobby?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The *Personal Information Protection Act* is not in the kb, so the answer must not state what it permits, and should say the question needs advice.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any statement about what PIPA permits or forbids; an assurance that publishing is fine because the list is a strata record.
