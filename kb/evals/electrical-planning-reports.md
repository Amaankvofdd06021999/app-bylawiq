# Evals for electrical planning reports

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: the deadline for an older building in Metro Vancouver

- **Question:** When do we need an electrical planning report? We are a 40 unit building in Burnaby, built in 2005.
- **Scope:** an existing strata corporation, non-phased strata plan, in a specified area.
- **Expected points:** On or before 31 December 2026, because the land is wholly or partly in the Metro Vancouver Regional District, which is a specified area. The fewer-than-5-lots exemption does not apply at 40 units.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s5.8`, `bc.spr.s5.7`, `bc.spa.s94.1`
- **Must not say:** 31 December 2028, which is the date for land wholly outside a specified area; a depreciation report deadline.

## Case 2: the same question outside the three regional districts

- **Question:** Same question, but we are in Kamloops.
- **Scope:** an existing strata corporation, non-phased strata plan, outside a specified area.
- **Expected points:** On or before 31 December 2028, because the land is wholly outside the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District and the Metro Vancouver Regional District.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s5.8`, `bc.spr.s5.7`
- **Must not say:** 31 December 2026; that the deadline is province-wide.

## Case 3: a small building

- **Question:** We have 4 strata lots. Do we need one at all?
- **Scope:** an existing strata corporation whose strata plan had fewer than 5 strata lots on 31 December 2023.
- **Expected points:** No. An existing strata corporation need not obtain an electrical planning report if the strata plan had fewer than 5 strata lots on 31 December 2023. The answer should test the count at that date, not today's.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s5.8`
- **Must not say:** That every strata corporation must obtain one; that the test is the current number of lots.

## Case 4: who can prepare it

- **Question:** Can we hire an electrician we already use for this?
- **Scope:** a strata corporation whose buildings are not all Part 9 buildings.
- **Expected points:** Only if they are a professional engineer or professional licensee engineering registered with Engineers and Geoscientists BC, or an applied science technologist or certified technician registered with ASTTBC. A journeyperson construction or industrial electrician qualifies only where every building in the strata plan is a Part 9 building.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s5.10`
- **Must not say:** That any licensed electrician qualifies; the depreciation report's list of qualified persons.

## Case 5: paying for it

- **Question:** Can we take the cost out of the contingency reserve fund, and what vote do we need?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Yes, and a majority vote is enough: an expenditure from the contingency reserve fund that is necessary to obtain an electrical planning report is in the majority-vote category, not the 3/4 category. The cost is also a purpose of the operating fund.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s96`, `bc.spa.s92`
- **Must not say:** That a 3/4 vote is required; that a special levy is the only route.

## Case 6: no grounding — our own deadline

- **Question:** When is our electrical planning report due?
- **Scope:** a building that has not uploaded its strata plan, its deposit date or its location.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state for this building. The answer may set out the rule — the two dates, the specified areas, the fewer-than-5-lots exemption and the 5 year rule for newer corporations — and must say the facts needed to apply it to this building are not available.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s5.8`
- **Must not say:** A date for this building; an assumption about where it is or when it was established.
