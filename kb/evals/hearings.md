# Evals for hearings

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: The deadline to hold a hearing

- **Question:** An owner wrote asking for a hearing. How long do we have?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Council must hold a council meeting to hear the applicant within four weeks after the request, where the request was in writing and stated the reason.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s34.1`
- **Must not say:** Any period other than four weeks; that council may decline the request.

## Case 2: The written decision after a hearing

- **Question:** Do we have to put the outcome of a hearing in writing?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Where the purpose of the hearing was to seek a decision of council, council must give the applicant a written decision within one week after the hearing. Where the hearing was not to seek a decision, that one-week duty does not arise.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s34.1`
- **Must not say:** That every hearing produces a written decision within a week, without the qualification.

## Case 3: Who may request a hearing

- **Question:** Can a tenant request a hearing, or only an owner?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** An owner or a tenant may request a hearing at a council meeting, by application in writing stating the reason for the request.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s34.1`
- **Must not say:** That only owners may request a hearing; that a particular form must be used.

## Case 4: Other owners wanting to watch

- **Question:** Other owners want to attend the hearing. Can they?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** Not that part of the meeting. Owners may attend council meetings as observers, except the portions dealing with a bylaw contravention hearing, or with any other matter where council considers that observers would unreasonably interfere with an individual's privacy.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw17`
- **Must not say:** That hearings are open to owners; that council has no power to exclude observers.

## Case 5: Holding the hearing remotely

- **Question:** Council meets by video. Does that satisfy a request to be heard in person?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** The standard bylaws let council meet by electronic means where everyone can communicate with each other, and deem members present in person. A hearing means an opportunity to be heard in person at a council meeting. The answer should set out both and note that the kb does not resolve whether a video hearing satisfies the definition.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw17`, `bc.spr.s7.2`
- **Must not say:** A confident yes or no; the deeming provision applied to the applicant as though it were about them rather than about council members.

## Case 6: No grounding: was our hearing adequate?

- **Question:** We held a hearing in June. Was it good enough?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no minutes or correspondence.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer sets out the requirements and says the records are not available.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s34.1`, `bc.spr.s7.2`
- **Must not say:** A conclusion that the hearing was or was not adequate.
