# Evals for council and governance

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Owners directing council

- **Question:** Owners passed a motion telling council to drop a fine. Must council comply?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. Owners may direct or restrict council by majority vote, but not where the direction interferes with council's discretion to determine, on the facts, whether a person contravened, whether to fine and how much, or whether to deny a recreational facility.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s27`
- **Must not say:** That a general meeting resolution binds council on a particular enforcement decision.

## Case 2: Conflict of interest

- **Question:** A council member's company wants the landscaping contract. What must happen?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The member must disclose the nature and extent of the interest fully and promptly, abstain from voting, and leave the meeting while it is discussed and voted on, unless asked to stay to provide information.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s32`
- **Must not say:** That disclosure alone is sufficient; that the member may vote if the contract is a good deal.

## Case 3: Paying council members

- **Question:** Can we pay the treasurer an honorarium?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Only if approved in advance in the budget, in the bylaws, or by a 3/4 vote at a general meeting.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s34`
- **Must not say:** That council can approve its own remuneration.

## Case 4: The standard of care

- **Question:** A council decision turned out badly. Are the members liable?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The statutory standard is to act honestly and in good faith with a view to the best interests of the corporation, and to exercise the care, diligence and skill of a reasonably prudent person in comparable circumstances; the standard bylaws protect a member who acts honestly and in good faith. The answer should set out the standard and not decide the case.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s31`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw22`
- **Must not say:** A conclusion about liability; a statement that a bad outcome is itself a breach.

## Case 5: Recording votes

- **Question:** Do we have to minute how each member voted?
- **Scope:** a building with the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** The standard bylaw requires the results of all votes at a council meeting to be recorded in the minutes. It does not in terms require each member's vote to be recorded individually.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw18`
- **Must not say:** A requirement to record individual votes, which the standard bylaw does not impose.

## Case 6: No grounding: was our council properly elected

- **Question:** Is our council validly constituted?
- **Scope:** a building with no uploaded minutes.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer sets out the election and eligibility requirements and says the records are not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** A conclusion either way.
