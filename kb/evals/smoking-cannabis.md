# Evals for smoking and cannabis

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Grandfathering smokers

- **Question:** We are passing a no-smoking bylaw. Are existing smokers protected?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not by the Act. Its grandfathering provision is about pets and applies only to pets. Any exemption for existing smokers exists only because the building's own bylaw creates one.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123`
- **Must not say:** That smokers are grandfathered the way pets are; a period of protection the Act does not give.

## Case 2: No smoking bylaw at all

- **Question:** Smoke is drifting from the unit below and we have no smoking bylaw. Can we do anything?
- **Scope:** a building with only the standard bylaws.
- **Expected points:** The complaint would be under the standard use bylaw — causing a nuisance or hazard, or unreasonably interfering with another person's use and enjoyment — not under a smoking bylaw, because there is none.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** That nothing can be done; a smoking contravention where no smoking bylaw exists.

## Case 3: Medical cannabis

- **Question:** An owner says they use cannabis medically and our bylaw bans smoking. What now?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Human Rights Code, so the bylaw's validity and its enforceability against this person are different questions. The Code is not in the kb, so the answer must not state the test, and should say the matter needs advice.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** The accommodation or undue hardship test; a conclusion either way.

## Case 4: Balconies

- **Question:** Does a no-smoking bylaw cover balconies?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** It depends on the bylaw's wording and on whether the balcony is limited common property or part of the strata lot, which the strata plan decides. The answer should set out the question rather than assume.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.sched.bylaw3`
- **Must not say:** A general rule that balconies are always covered, or never are.

## Case 5: No grounding: provincial or municipal smoking law

- **Question:** Isn't smoking already illegal indoors under provincial law?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The kb holds the Strata Property Act and Regulation, which say nothing about smoking, and no public health or municipal legislation.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any provincial or municipal smoking restriction stated as fact.
