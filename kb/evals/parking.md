# Evals for parking

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: How long a stall can be assigned

- **Question:** We assigned a common property stall to an owner ten years ago. Is it theirs?
- **Scope:** a stall that is common property and not limited common property.
- **Expected points:** No. Exclusive use of common property can be given for no more than one year at a time, renewable on changed terms and cancellable on reasonable notice. A permanent allocation requires designating the stall limited common property.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s76`, `bc.spa.s74`
- **Must not say:** That long use creates a permanent right; that council can grant a stall indefinitely.

## Case 2: Charging for a stall

- **Question:** Can we charge $50 a month for a visitor stall?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Only under a bylaw, or a rule that has been ratified, and only if the amount is reasonable. A user fee for common property is otherwise prohibited.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s110`, `bc.spr.s6.9`
- **Must not say:** That council can set a fee by resolution alone; a figure said to be standard.

## Case 3: Turning visitor parking into assigned parking

- **Question:** Can council reassign the visitor stalls to owners?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** It has to ask whether that is a significant change in the use of common property, which needs a 3/4 vote unless immediate change is needed for safety or to prevent significant loss or damage.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s71`, `bc.spa.s76`
- **Must not say:** That council may do it alone without considering whether it is a significant change.

## Case 4: Taking a stall away as a penalty

- **Question:** Can we remove someone's stall for repeatedly breaking the parking rules?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Reassigning a stall is not one of the Act's three enforcement options, which are a fine, remedying a contravention and denying a recreational facility. Where the stall is a year-to-year permission, cancellation on reasonable notice is a different power and should not be used as a disguised penalty.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s76`, `bc.spa.s129`
- **Must not say:** That a stall can be withdrawn as a sanction.

## Case 5: No grounding: whose stall is it

- **Question:** Is stall 14 common property or part of the strata lot?
- **Scope:** a building that has not uploaded its strata plan.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The answer explains that the strata plan decides it and says the plan is not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** An assumption either way; an inference from who has been using it.
