# Evals for common property use

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Significant change

- **Question:** We want to replace the lobby furniture and repaint in a new colour. Does that need a vote?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** It depends whether it is a significant change in the use or appearance of common property. If it is, a 3/4 vote is needed unless immediate change is necessary for safety or to prevent significant loss or damage. The answer should frame the question rather than decide it.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s71`
- **Must not say:** A rule that painting is always, or never, a significant change.

## Case 2: Charging for the amenity room

- **Question:** Can we charge owners to book the party room?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Only under a bylaw or a ratified rule, and only a reasonable amount, which may be fixed or set on a reasonable basis such as recovering operating or maintenance costs or the duration of use.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s110`, `bc.spr.s6.9`
- **Must not say:** That council may set a fee by resolution.

## Case 3: Exclusive use of a storage area

- **Question:** Can we let one owner use an empty common storage room permanently?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Not permanently. Exclusive use of common property that is not limited common property can be given for no more than one year at a time, renewable and cancellable on reasonable notice; a permanent arrangement means designating it limited common property.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s76`
- **Must not say:** That a long-term licence is available.

## Case 4: Access

- **Question:** An owner with a fenced limited common property yard will not let us in to inspect the drainage.
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** An owner with the right to use common property, including limited common property, or common assets must allow the corporation reasonable access to exercise its powers and perform its duties.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s77`
- **Must not say:** That the corporation may enter a strata lot on the same basis; the two are governed differently.

## Case 5: No grounding: what is common property here

- **Question:** Is the rooftop deck common property?
- **Scope:** a building with no uploaded strata plan.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The strata plan decides it and is not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** An assumption from how the space is used.
