# Evals for rentals

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Capping the number of rentals

- **Question:** Can we pass a bylaw limiting rentals to 20% of the units?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A strata corporation must not screen tenants, set screening criteria, require approval of tenants, require terms in tenancy agreements or otherwise restrict the rental of a strata lot, and the provisions that used to allow rental restriction bylaws were repealed in 2022.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s141`
- **Must not say:** That a rental cap is allowed with a 3/4 vote; any reference to sections 142 to 145 as though they were in force; an exemption or hardship procedure under the repealed scheme.

## Case 2: An old rental restriction bylaw still on file

- **Question:** Our registered bylaws still contain a rental restriction from 2015. Can we enforce it?
- **Scope:** a building whose filed bylaws include a rental restriction.
- **Expected points:** No. A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act, and a rental restriction contravenes section 141. Remaining on file does not make it enforceable.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s141`, `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** That a filed bylaw is valid until repealed; that the bylaw binds owners who bought while it was in force.

## Case 3: Landlord did not give the tenant the bylaws

- **Question:** A landlord never gave their tenant the bylaws or the signed notice. What follows?
- **Scope:** a residential strata lot.
- **Expected points:** The tenant is still bound by the bylaws and rules, but may end the tenancy without penalty within 90 days of learning of the failure, and the landlord must then pay reasonable moving expenses up to one month's rent. The landlord also had to give the corporation the signed notice within two weeks of renting.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s146`
- **Must not say:** That the tenant is not bound by the bylaws; a penalty against the landlord that the Act does not provide.

## Case 4: Fining a tenant

- **Question:** We want to fine a tenant for a bylaw breach. Anything different from fining an owner?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Yes. Notice of the complaint must also go to the tenant's landlord and to the owner before any fine, and the written decision goes to them too. The fine may be collected from the tenant, the landlord and the owner, but not more than the total once over.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s135`, `bc.spa.s131`
- **Must not say:** That the tenant alone is notified; that the corporation may collect the full fine from both the tenant and the owner.

## Case 5: Whether a tenant may vote

- **Question:** Can a tenant vote at our annual general meeting?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** Only if the landlord has assigned that right and the corporation has received written notice naming the tenant, the powers assigned and the period. The answer should say to check for an assignment notice rather than assume.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s147`
- **Must not say:** That tenants may never vote; that a tenant may vote simply by attending.

## Case 6: No grounding: ending a tenancy under the Residential Tenancy Act

- **Question:** How much notice does the Residential Tenancy Act require for a notice to end tenancy for cause?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The Act makes a repeated or continuing contravention of a reasonable and significant bylaw an event allowing such a notice, but the *Residential Tenancy Act* is not in the kb, so its notice periods cannot be stated.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s137`, `bc.spa.s138`
- **Must not say:** Any notice period in days or months; any Residential Tenancy Branch procedure.
