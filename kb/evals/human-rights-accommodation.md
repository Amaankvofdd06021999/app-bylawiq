# Evals for human rights and accommodation

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses.

## Case 1: Age restriction floor

- **Question:** Can we pass a bylaw restricting residence to people over 40?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A bylaw must not restrict the age of persons who may reside in a strata lot, except a requirement that one or more residents have reached a specified age of not less than 55.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123.1`
- **Must not say:** Any minimum age below 55; that any age restriction is permitted with a 3/4 vote.

## Case 2: Caregivers and children in a 55+ building

- **Question:** Our bylaw is 55+. A resident's caregiver is 40 and her child lives there. Does the bylaw apply to them?
- **Scope:** a building with a valid 55+ bylaw.
- **Expected points:** No. The age requirement does not apply to a caregiver residing in the lot to care for a dependent resident, nor to the prescribed classes, which include a child one of whose caregivers is a specified resident, a person 19 or over residing with a specified resident who was their caregiver before they turned 19, and the spouse of a specified resident.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123.2`, `bc.spr.s7.01`
- **Must not say:** That the bylaw applies to everyone in the lot; that the caregiver needs an accommodation request.

## Case 3: Guide dog

- **Question:** Do we have to accommodate a guide dog, or can we require proof of disability?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No accommodation analysis arises: a bylaw prohibiting or restricting animals does not apply to a guide dog or service dog, or to a dog of a retired guide or service dog team whose member is an owner, tenant or occupant. Proof of disability is not the test.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s123`
- **Must not say:** A requirement for medical evidence; an accommodation analysis.

## Case 4: The limit on any bylaw

- **Question:** Our bylaw was properly passed and filed. Does that make it enforceable against everyone?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** No. A bylaw is not enforceable to the extent that it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the Human Rights Code or any other enactment or law, so validity and enforceability against a particular person are different questions.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** That a validly filed bylaw is enforceable against everyone.

## Case 5: No grounding: the accommodation test

- **Question:** Would accommodating this request be undue hardship?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The Human Rights Code is not in the kb, so the test cannot be stated or applied, and the answer should say the question needs advice.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s121`
- **Must not say:** The undue hardship test; any conclusion on the request.
