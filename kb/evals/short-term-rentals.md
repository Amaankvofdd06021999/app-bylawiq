# Evals for short-term rentals

Each case lists the question, the scope it runs in, the points a correct answer makes, the kb ids it must cite, and what it must not say. A case whose expected result is "no grounding found" is testing that the assistant declines rather than guesses; treating it as a failure to answer would be the wrong reading.

## Case 1: Fining $1,000 without amending the bylaws

- **Question:** Our bylaw bans Airbnb. Can we fine $1,000 a contravention?
- **Scope:** a building that has not amended the standard fine bylaws.
- **Expected points:** No. The Regulation's $1,000 is a ceiling, and the corporation may only fine up to the maximum set out in its own bylaws. With the standard bylaws unamended that is $50.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s132`, `bc.spr.s7.1`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw23`
- **Must not say:** $1,000 as an automatic entitlement; that the Regulation overrides the building's own bylaw maximum.

## Case 2: Fining daily

- **Question:** Can we fine every day while a unit is being listed nightly?
- **Scope:** a building that has not amended the standard continuing contravention bylaw.
- **Expected points:** Not yet. Daily fines are available only for a bylaw prohibiting or limiting use for remuneration as vacation, travel or temporary accommodation, and only if the building's bylaws set that frequency; the standard bylaw allows a fine every 7 days.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spr.s7.1`, `bc.spa.sched.bylaw24`, `bc.spa.s132`
- **Must not say:** That daily fines follow automatically from having a short-term accommodation bylaw.

## Case 3: Whether section 141 defeats a short-term accommodation bylaw

- **Question:** Does section 141 mean we cannot ban short-term rentals at all?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The kb does not settle it, and the answer should say so. Section 141 bars restricting the rental of a strata lot; the Regulation sets a higher fine for a bylaw prohibiting or limiting use for vacation, travel or temporary accommodation, so it assumes such a bylaw can exist. Nothing in the Act or Regulation draws the line.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s141`, `bc.spr.s7.1`
- **Must not say:** A confident yes or no; a distinction between rental and licence presented as settled law.

## Case 4: Advertising versus accommodation

- **Question:** The listing is still up but we cannot prove anyone stayed. Can we fine?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** It depends which bylaw was contravened. A bylaw against advertising and a bylaw against the accommodation are different bylaws needing different evidence, and the fine must match the bylaw actually breached. The complaint and answer process applies either way.
- **Must cite:** `bc.spa.s132`, `bc.spa.s135`
- **Must not say:** That a listing proves a contravention of a bylaw prohibiting the accommodation.

## Case 5: No grounding: provincial registration

- **Question:** Does the owner need a provincial short-term rental registration number?
- **Scope:** any building.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state. The Strata Property Act and Regulation say nothing about the provincial short-term rental registration scheme and it is not in the kb.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** Any registration requirement, exemption or deadline; any municipal licensing requirement.

## Case 6: No grounding: whether this building has such a bylaw

- **Question:** Are short-term rentals banned in this building?
- **Scope:** a building that has uploaded no bylaws.
- **Expected points:** The "no grounding found" state for the building-specific part. The answer may explain what such a bylaw would look like, but must say the building's bylaws are not available.
- **Must cite:** nothing
- **Must not say:** An assumption either way about this building's bylaws.
