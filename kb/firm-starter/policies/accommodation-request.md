# Accommodation requests

> **Not yet approved.** A BC-licensed lawyer has to review this before it is sent to anyone. The steps below cite the provision that requires them; the wording does not come from any approved precedent.

> **Read this limit first.** The *Human Rights Code* is **not in the knowledge base** (research task
> A5). This policy is a process for handling a request and getting it to the right people. It does
> not state the legal test for discrimination, accommodation or undue hardship, and nothing in it
> should be used as though it did.

## Purpose

To make sure a request that engages human rights is recognised early, handled consistently, and put
in front of counsel before a decision is made or a penalty imposed.

## When it applies

Whenever a person asks for an exception to a bylaw, rule or practice, or objects to one, on a
ground connected to disability, family status, age, religion or another protected characteristic —
however the request is worded, and whether or not the words "accommodation" or "human rights" are
used. It applies as much when the request arrives inside a complaint response as when it arrives on
its own.

## Why it matters under the Act

A bylaw is unenforceable to the extent that it contravenes the Act, the regulations, the
*Human Rights Code* or another enactment or law [bc.spa.s121]. So a bylaw the building has validly
passed and filed may still not be enforceable against a particular person, and enforcing it anyway
is the risk this policy exists to prevent.

Two situations are already answered by the Act and do **not** need a case-by-case analysis: a pet
that was living with an owner, tenant or occupant lawfully before a pet bylaw was passed, and a
guide dog, service dog or retired guide or service dog team dog [bc.spa.s123].

## The steps

1. **Recognise and record.** Log the request, the date, and what is being asked for. Do not decide
   it at the counter.
2. **Pause enforcement** on the matter the request relates to, and diary any deadline that is
   running, including a hearing request [bc.spa.s34.1] or a complaint response [bc.spa.s135].
3. **Acknowledge in writing** within {{acknowledgement_days}} days, saying the request has been
   received, who is handling it, and what happens next. Do not indicate an outcome.
4. **Ask only for what is needed** to understand the request, and say why each thing is needed.
   Do not ask for a diagnosis.
5. **Refer to counsel** before council decides. The test to be applied is not in the kb, and the
   consequences of getting it wrong fall on the strata corporation.
6. **Council decides**, with the advice, and records the decision and its reasons in the minutes.
7. **Respond in writing**, and keep the file.

## What must not happen

- Enforcing the bylaw while the request is unresolved.
- Asking a person with a guide dog or service dog to prove a disability [bc.spa.s123].
- Treating a grandfathered pet as requiring any accommodation analysis at all: the Act already
  answers it [bc.spa.s123].
- Council or a manager deciding the human rights question without advice.
- Discussing the request where other owners can hear it.
