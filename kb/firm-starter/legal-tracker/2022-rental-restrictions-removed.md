# Rental restriction bylaws stopped working (2022)

> **Not yet approved.** A BC-licensed lawyer has to review this before it is sent to anyone. The steps below cite the provision that requires them; the wording does not come from any approved precedent.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What changed** | Section 141 of the Strata Property Act now reads "No restriction of rentals by strata corporation" and provides that a strata corporation must not screen tenants, establish screening criteria, require the approval of tenants, require the insertion of terms in tenancy agreements or otherwise restrict the rental of a strata lot [bc.spa.s141]. Sections 142 to 145, which carried the rental restriction bylaw machinery, were repealed. |
| **When** | The BC Laws consolidation records the repeal of sections 142 to 145 as `2022-41-19` and of sections 139 to 140 as `2022-41-17`. **The in-force date is not in the kb**: the consolidation does not give per-section dates, and the Tables of Legislative Changes have not been read (open questions 21, 24 and 25). Do not state a date until it has been checked. |
| **Who is affected** | Every residential strata corporation with a rental restriction bylaw still on file, and every owner who was told they could not rent. |
| **What to do** | Stop enforcing rental restriction bylaws: a bylaw is unenforceable to the extent it contravenes the Act [bc.spa.s121], and remaining on file does not save it. Review the registered bylaws for a cap on rented lots, a waiting list, a rental pool, an owner-occupancy requirement, a minimum period of ownership before renting, or council approval of tenants. Check whether any fine was imposed under such a bylaw. Raise repeal at the next general meeting so the filed bylaws stop misleading owners and buyers. |
| **What is still allowed** | Bylaws that require information about a tenancy rather than restricting it, and the landlord and tenant duties the Act itself imposes. See [building.bylaw-pattern.rentals] and [topic.rentals]. |
| **Source** | The Act as consolidated, read 2026-09-30. Repeal citations from the importer's `omitted[]` record in `law/bc/acts/strata-property-act/source.json`. |

## Why this is the first tracker entry

It is the change most likely to be sitting unnoticed in a building's filed bylaws, and the one where
continuing to enforce creates the clearest exposure. It is also the only change this kb can evidence
so far: filling the tracker properly needs the Tables of Legislative Changes (task A2) and the
decision summaries (phase C3), both of which are blocked.
