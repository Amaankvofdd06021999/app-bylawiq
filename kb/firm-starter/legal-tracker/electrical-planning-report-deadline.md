# Electrical planning reports are due 31 December 2026 in the Lower Mainland and Capital region

> **Not yet approved.** A BC-licensed lawyer has to review this before it is sent to anyone. The
> steps below cite the provision that requires them; the wording does not come from any approved
> precedent.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What changed** | A strata corporation must obtain an **electrical planning report** from a qualified person by dates the Regulation sets [bc.spa.s94.1]. This is a new and separate obligation from the depreciation report, with its own deadline, its own qualified person and its own contents. |
| **When** | For a strata corporation established on or before 31 December 2023 with a strata plan that is not phased: **31 December 2026** if the land is wholly or partly in the Capital Regional District, the Fraser Valley Regional District or the Metro Vancouver Regional District, and **31 December 2028** if it is wholly outside all three. Islands within the Capital Regional District and Metro Vancouver that are reachable only by air or boat are outside the specified area [bc.spr.s5.8, bc.spr.s5.7]. A phased plan can have several deadlines, one per qualifying phase [bc.spr.s5.9]. A newer corporation has 5 years from deposit [bc.spr.s5.8]. Unlike the rental restriction entry in this tracker, these dates are in the Regulation's own text, so they do not depend on the Tables of Legislative Changes. |
| **Who is affected** | Every strata corporation with 5 or more strata lots. A strata plan with fewer than 5 strata lots on 31 December 2023, or at deposit for a newer corporation, is exempt [bc.spr.s5.8]. |
| **What to do** | Sort the portfolio into three groups: due 31 December 2026, due 31 December 2028, and exempt. The sort needs each building's regional district, its establishment date, whether its plan is phased, and its lot count at 31 December 2023 — not today's count. For the first group, budget and engage now: the report needs an on-site understanding of the electrical system, and a qualified person is a registered professional engineer or professional licensee engineering, or an ASTTBC applied science technologist or certified technician. A journeyperson construction or industrial electrician qualifies **only** where every building in the strata plan is a Part 9 building [bc.spr.s5.10]. |
| **How it is paid for** | The cost is a purpose of the operating fund [bc.spa.s92], and an expenditure from the contingency reserve fund necessary to obtain the report can be approved by a **majority** vote, not a 3/4 vote [bc.spa.s96]. Councils that assume 3/4 will set up a harder meeting than they need. |
| **The second effect** | The report date is the gate on EV charging requests. An owner cannot ask the corporation to approve alterations to common property for EV charging until the corporation has obtained its first electrical planning report or until the date that report was due, whichever is earlier; where no report is required, the date is 31 December 2026 [bc.spr.s5.4]. Buildings that obtain the report will start receiving requests, each carrying a 3 month decision deadline and a bar on unreasonable refusal. See [topic.ev-charging]. |
| **Keep it** | Any electrical planning report obtained is a record the corporation must retain [bc.spa.s35], permanently [bc.spr.s4.1]. |
| **Source** | Act and Regulation as consolidated, read 2026-10-01. The deadline provisions were enacted by B.C. Reg. 261/2023, as the Regulation's own history notes record. |

## Why this is in the tracker

Two reasons. It is the only obligation in the kb with a deadline inside the next three months, and
the sorting work it needs — regional district, establishment date, phasing, lot count at a past date
— is portfolio work a firm has to start before it can advise any single building. It also opens the
EV request process, so a building that meets the deadline should expect the next piece of work
immediately after.
