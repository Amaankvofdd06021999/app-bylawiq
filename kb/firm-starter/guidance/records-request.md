# Responding to a records request

> **Not yet approved.** A BC-licensed lawyer has to review this before it is sent to anyone. The steps below cite the provision that requires them; the wording does not come from any approved precedent.

The policy is `firm.policy.records-request`. This is the working method, in the order the clock
runs.

## Day 0: the request arrives

Write down the date. The deadlines run from receipt: **one week** for the bylaws or rules, **two
weeks** for everything else [bc.spa.s36], and **one week** for an Information Certificate
[bc.spa.s59].

Check standing: an owner; a tenant who has been assigned the landlord's right to inspect and obtain
copies; someone authorised in writing by either; or a former owner or tenant, for records relating
to their own period [bc.spa.s36, bc.spa.s147].

Do not ask what it is for. The Act makes no reason a condition of access [bc.spa.s36].

## Day 1: map the request to the Act's list

The right of access runs to the records the corporation must prepare and retain [bc.spa.s35]. Go
item by item and put each into one of three piles:

- **On the list.** Minutes of general and council meetings with the results of votes; the list of
  council members; the list of owners, mortgagees who filed a request for notification, tenants and
  landlord-to-tenant assignments; books of account; the strata plan and amendments; the Act and
  regulations; the bylaws and rules; resolutions on changes to common property; waivers and consents;
  written contracts; decisions of an arbitrator, judge or the tribunal and legal opinions; budgets
  and financial statements; tax returns; correspondence sent or received by the corporation and
  council; bank statements and cancelled cheques; Information Certificates; owner developer
  documents; depreciation reports; major-item reports; electrical planning and EV reports
  [bc.spa.s35].
- **Not on the list.** Say so, and say why, rather than ignoring the item.
- **Not sure.** Counsel, before answering.

## Day 2 to 5: gather, and deal with what is missing

If a record cannot be found, check whether it should still exist. Retention runs from permanent
(strata plan, common property resolutions, decisions and legal opinions, depreciation reports,
electrical planning reports, EV reports), through six years (minutes, books of account, waivers and
consents, budgets and financial statements, tax returns, bank records, Information Certificates, and
contracts and insurance policies from when they end), to two years (correspondence), and current
copies only for the council list, the owner and tenant lists, the Act and regulations, the bylaws
and rules, and council contact details [bc.spr.s4.1].

Tell the requester which of those applies. "We no longer hold it, and were not required to" is an
answer; silence is not.

## Before the deadline: produce

By inspection or by copies, as asked [bc.spa.s36]. Send a covering note listing what is enclosed,
what is not and why, and the date.

## Where it goes wrong

- Treating everything as a two week job and missing the one week deadline for bylaws and rules.
- Asking the purpose, and delaying while waiting for an answer.
- Refusing a former owner records from their own period of ownership.
- Refusing a tenant, without checking for an assignment [bc.spa.s147].
- Sending nothing at all for the items that are not held, so the response looks like a refusal.
- Waiting for approval before circulating council minutes, which is a separate two week duty that
  does not depend on anyone asking [bc.spa.sched.bylaw19].
