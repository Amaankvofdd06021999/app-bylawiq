# Handling a human rights accommodation request

> **Not yet approved.** A BC-licensed lawyer has to review this before it is sent to anyone. The steps below cite the provision that requires them; the wording does not come from any approved precedent.

> **The limit on this note.** The *Human Rights Code* is not in the knowledge base (research task
> A5), and no decisions are either. So this explains **how to handle** a request and what the Strata
> Property Act contributes. It does not state when accommodation is required, what undue hardship
> means, or how a request should be decided. Those need counsel.

## What the Act contributes

One provision, and it is the important one: a bylaw is not enforceable to the extent that it
contravenes the Act, the regulations, the *Human Rights Code* or any other enactment or law
[bc.spa.s121]. A validly passed and filed bylaw can therefore be unenforceable against a particular
person. Enforcement is the risk, not the request.

Two questions the Act answers outright, so they are not accommodation questions at all
[bc.spa.s123]:

- A pet that was living with an owner, tenant or occupant immediately before a pet bylaw was passed,
  lawfully at the time, and that goes on living with them: the bylaw does not apply to it.
- A guide dog, a service dog, or a dog of a retired guide or service dog team where the team member
  is an owner, tenant or occupant: a bylaw prohibiting or restricting animals, or their access, does
  not apply. No disability needs to be shown, because the exemption turns on what the dog is.

Anything beyond those two goes through the process below.

## Recognising a request

It will rarely arrive labelled. Treat as a request any of: an owner saying a bylaw affects them
because of a medical condition; a resident asking to keep an animal that is not a guide or service
dog for support reasons; a request to be exempt from a rule because of a child, a caregiver or
religious observance; a complaint that the building's conduct is discriminatory. A request made in
answer to an enforcement letter is still a request.

## What to do

1. **Stop the enforcement clock on that matter**, and tell the person you have done so. Fining
   someone while their request is unresolved is the sequence that causes the harm.
2. **Acknowledge in writing**, promptly, saying what happens next and who is handling it — without
   hinting at an outcome.
3. **Ask for what is needed and no more.** What limitation needs to be worked around, and what would
   meet it. Not a diagnosis, not a medical history.
4. **Keep it confidential.** Not in minutes that circulate to owners, not in the lobby, not
   discussed with the complainant.
5. **Get advice before council decides.** The test is not in the kb.
6. **Answer in writing**, with reasons, and record the decision.

## What not to do

- Ask a person with a guide dog or service dog for proof of disability [bc.spa.s123].
- Treat a grandfathered pet as needing accommodation analysis [bc.spa.s123].
- Tell the person the bylaw is valid and therefore enforceable. Validity and enforceability against
  this person are different questions [bc.spa.s121].
- Proceed to a fine while the request sits unanswered [bc.spa.s135].
- Decide undue hardship in-house.
